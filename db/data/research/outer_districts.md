# Vienna Outer Districts — Research Pass

Deep-dive into outer districts thinly represented in the master catalog. All entries outdoor, free, GPS-friendly, reachable by U-Bahn / tram / bus. Holocaust, Nazi-era, plague, gallows, composer-deathbed memorials excluded. Bias toward layered / surprising / aesthetic.

Format follows master catalog: `### Name (address)` — 2-3 sentence hook — *Sources*.

---

## District 1050 — Margareten

### Margaretenhof (Margaretengürtel 84–86)
A late-1880s Gründerzeit "people's palace" 35 years older than Reumannhof next door — Theophil Hansen-school historicism applied to working-class housing before Red Vienna existed. The brick-and-stucco façade has stepped gables, oriel turrets and a corner statue of St. Margaret slaying her dragon (the district's namesake). A capitalist-era prototype the socialists later copied at scale a block south.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Schloss Margarethen (Margaretenplatz 2)
A small Renaissance castle (16th-century, rebuilt 1727) hiding inside a courtyard of ordinary apartment buildings — once a country seat of the Habsburgs' chamberlain when this was open land beyond the walls. Above the gate sits a relief of the dragon-slaying Saint Margaret; the inner courtyard with its old well is publicly accessible during the day. A pocket castle most Viennese have never seen.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Bettelstudentbrunnen (Margaretenplatz)
A 1929 cast-stone fountain showing the protagonist of Carl Millöcker's operetta *Der Bettelstudent* — the "Beggar Student" — perched on the basin in patched trousers, lyre in hand. Operetta as municipal sculpture: the only fountain in Vienna celebrating a fictional broke musician rather than a saint or a Habsburg.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Hundertwasserbrunnen (Siebenbrunnenplatz)
The "Seven Wells" square once had seven natural springs; the 1846 fountain marks the spot where Vienna's first municipal piped water was tapped before the Alpine pipeline replaced it. Friedensreich Hundertwasser donated a redesign of the surrounds late in life — irregular tile patterns ring the original stone basin.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Kaiserstraße / Schloss Schönbrunner Schlossstraße axis
Kaiserstraße was laid out as the Habsburg processional route between the Hofburg and Schönbrunn — Maria Theresa's carriages rolled through here twice daily in summer. The street still runs dead-straight on its original 18th-century survey line; the houses are Biedermeier, the trams are red, and the axis terminates at a palace gate 4 km away.
*Sources: outer_districts*

## District 1060 — Mariahilf

### Raimund-Brunnen (Wallgasse / Raimundtheater)
A bronze of playwright Ferdinand Raimund as the genie from his own *Der Verschwender* ("The Spendthrift"), 1898 — Raimund invented Viennese magic-comedy theatre a generation before Nestroy. The theatre he gave his name to (1893) sits behind, still staging musicals. A statue of a fictional spirit conjured by a real writer in front of the building named for him.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Theater an der Wien — Papageno relief (Millöckergasse side)
The famous "Papagenotor" everyone photographs from the Wienzeile is matched by a quieter side façade on Millöckergasse with the original 1801 stone relief of Schikaneder-as-Papageno surrounded by his three sons — the autograph of the man who commissioned *Die Zauberflöte* and starred in it. The Wienzeile side is the show; this side is the signature.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Capistrankanzel-Säule corner (Mariahilferstraße / Stiftgasse)
On the corner above eye level: a small Baroque column with an angel blowing a trumpet, marking the spot where Franciscan friar John of Capistrano allegedly preached in the 1450s rallying troops against the Turks. Vienna has half a dozen of these forgotten preacher-columns — this one is the most overlooked.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Stiftskaserne porch (Mariahilfer Straße 24)
The 1739 Baroque chapel-and-barracks complex by Andrea Altomonte cuts a chunk out of Vienna's main shopping street — soldiers and saints sharing one wall. The chapel façade has gilded military trophies stuck onto the saints' niches: an 18th-century mashup of altar and arsenal.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Fillgraderstiege (Fillgradergasse, off Theobaldgasse)
A Jugendstil staircase by Max Hegele (1907) connecting two streets on Mariahilf's steep edge — wrought-iron lanterns, sinuous curves, lily-of-the-valley balustrades. A miniature Strudlhofstiege most guidebooks miss; Hegele also designed the Zentralfriedhof's main chapel.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Mariahilfer Straße / Andreasgasse — Alfons-Petzold-Hof
An unsung 1925 Red Vienna Hof named after a worker-poet who died at 41 of tuberculosis — Petzold wrote socialist verse in a language self-taught from public libraries. The building above the U3 Neubaugasse station displays bronze relief portraits of him under the cornice.
*Sources: outer_districts*

## District 1070 — Neubau

### Burggasse-Stadthalle Stadtbahn station (Burggasse 113 / Urban-Loritz-Platz)
Otto Wagner's surviving Stadtbahn station entry pavilion (1898) for the Gürtel viaduct line — apple-green ironwork, tiled half-dome, sunflower wreaths along the gable. Now a U6 stop where commuters pour past Wagner's last great pavilion every 90 seconds without looking up.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Volkstheater façade (Neustiftgasse 1)
Fellner & Helmer's 1889 theatre — the same Habsburg-era duo who built theatres in 47 European cities — designed to be "the people's Burgtheater." A relief of muses above the entry, four allegorical figures on the parapet (Tragedy, Comedy, Truth, Beauty). Locals named it *Deutsches Volkstheater* in protest at the elitism of the official Burgtheater.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Siebensternplatz fountain & the Seven Stars
A small triangular square named after a 17th-century house sign showing seven stars — Vienna's Masonic lodge "Zu den sieben Sternen" (Mozart was a member elsewhere, but the symbol travels). The modern fountain places seven bronze stars in the cobbles around the basin; a coffee-house Masonic Easter egg.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### MQ Library "white" balcony (MuseumsQuartier, north court)
On the upper level of the MQ courtyard, a long white concrete balcony cantilevers out over the Burggasse — designed for nothing in particular, no railing on the inner side, just a place to lean and watch. Locals call it "die Wiese" (the meadow). One of the city's best free west-facing sunset spots.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Ulrichsplatz (Sankt-Ulrichs-Platz)
A small triangular Baroque square hidden between Neubaugasse and Burggasse, anchored by the 1721 Ulrichskirche — built on the site of Vienna's oldest documented suburb (founded c. 1211). A village square stranded inside the modern city; the houses around it are mostly 17th–18th century. Walk in and the traffic noise drops.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Amerlinghaus & Schrankgasse 7
A 1715 Biedermeier house with a glassed-in courtyard "Pawlatschen" (open wooden gallery walkways) — Friedrich von Amerling, society portraitist of Sisi's mother, was born here in 1803. The courtyard is one of the last surviving wooden-gallery houses in central Vienna and is publicly walk-through.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Kirchberggasse — house "Zur goldenen Krone"
A surviving 17th-century inn sign on an unremarkable apartment block: a gilded crown above the doorway, marking what was once a roadside coaching inn just outside the Stadtmauer. Vienna's house-name system predates street numbers; about 200 medieval signs survive, this is one of the prettiest.
*Sources: outer_districts*

## District 1080 — Josefstadt

### Piaristenkirche & Jodok-Fink-Platz
Lukas von Hildebrandt's 1716 Baroque church on a quiet cobbled square — twin onion-domed towers, Maulbertsch frescoes inside (free entry). Anton Bruckner played the organ here. The square outside is a hidden village green with chestnut trees and a Marian column; almost no tourists ever find it. One of Vienna's most photogenic Baroque ensembles.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Theater in der Josefstadt (Josefstädter Straße 26)
Vienna's oldest continuously-operating theatre (1788) — Beethoven conducted his *Weihe des Hauses* overture at the reopening on 3 October 1822, the only opening of any theatre Beethoven ever conducted. Max Reinhardt redesigned the interior in 1924 and ran it until 1937. The chandelier still dims by being slowly lowered into the ceiling, a Reinhardt invention.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Schönborn Park (Laudongasse 15–19)
The former private garden of Palais Schönborn (now the Volkskundemuseum) — Hildebrandt designed the palace in 1706 for an Imperial Vice-Chancellor. The park is a flat green rectangle with old-growth plane trees, gravel paths and a single Baroque garden pavilion. Free; locals use it as their living room.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Lange Gasse — Beethoven trail
Beethoven moved 68 times in 35 years in Vienna and Lange Gasse claims at least four addresses — Nr. 26 (1815), Nr. 46 (1821), Nr. 70. The whole 1.2-km Biedermeier corridor is a string of plaques and faded house numbers tracking his serial relocation. Walk it and you're tracing a 19th-century music gypsy.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Strozzigasse villas
A short street running uphill toward the Rathaus — the houses are Biedermeier-to-Gründerzeit with elaborate stucco caryatids and Atlantes holding up bay windows. The 1880s building boom rolled through here when industrialists wanted addresses close to the Ring but cheaper than inside it. A free Gründerzeit façade museum.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Skodagasse arcade
A short curving street named after surgeon Joseph Skoda (the "Skoda phenomenon" in chest medicine is named for his percussion technique). Lined with 1880s pharmacies and medical-instrument shops — the historic apothecary's quarter of Vienna's medical district. The Allgemeines Krankenhaus is one block north.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Rathauspark — Engerthbrunnen
On the northwest corner of Rathauspark (technically straddling 1080) sits a modest 1899 fountain dedicated to Wilhelm von Engerth, the railway engineer who built the Semmering line — the world's first mountain railway and a UNESCO site. A small Symbolist bronze of a steam locomotive emerging from rock; an engineer's monument shaped like the engineering.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Hamerlingpark (Hamerlingplatz)
A small landscaped square named for poet Robert Hamerling, with a 1900 bronze bust on a Jugendstil pedestal at the center and curved gravel paths. The park is barely bigger than a tennis court but functions as the social hinge of the Josefstadt — terrace cafés spill out around it.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Alser Straße — Trinitarian Order corner
At the Lange Gasse / Alser Straße corner stands a Baroque relief of the Holy Trinity above a former monastery gate — the Trinitarian Order ransomed Christian slaves from North African pirates in the 17th–18th centuries, an unusual order whose Vienna monastery operated a public ransom collection box here. The relief survives; the function does not.
*Sources: outer_districts*

## District 1100 — Favoriten

### Reumannplatz & Eismarillenknödel cluster
The square named after Vienna's first socialist mayor (Jakob Reumann, elected 1919) — anchor of working-class Favoriten. The 1980 marble fountain by Anton Hanak shows nude figures striving upward toward a sun; the 1953 Tichy ice cream shop on the corner invented the *Eismarillenknödel* (apricot dumpling reimagined as ice cream) that Viennese still queue for.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Viktor-Adler-Markt (Viktor-Adler-Platz)
Vienna's most Anatolian street market — börek, Balkan cheese, Turkish bridal-veil shops, no tourists. Named for the founder of Austrian social democracy. The market hall building (1899) has cast-iron pillars and an arched glass roof; the open-air stalls spread along Quellenstraße. Saturday mornings are the moment.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Per-Albin-Hansson-Siedlung (Per-Albin-Hansson-Straße)
A 1947–51 garden-suburb of single-family terraced houses on the southern fringe — postwar utopia built on the Swedish *folkhemmet* model, named for the Swedish PM whose welfare-state writings inspired it. Low pitched roofs, garden plots, white-rendered walls, a single church at the center. A working-class arcadia 20 minutes from Stephansplatz.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Böhmischer Prater (Laaer-Berg-Straße)
A miniature wooden funfair tucked into the south-eastern hills, founded 1882 by Bohemian brick-workers who needed weekend amusement after their shifts. Has a 1909 carved-horse carousel still in operation, a wooden swing-ride from 1900, and Kreidl's beer garden. The world's smallest still-functioning Belle Époque amusement park.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Wasserturm Favoriten (Windtenstraße 3)
A 67-metre brick water tower (1899) with Romanesque-revival turrets, more castle than utility — Vienna's most ornate piece of infrastructure. The dome inside houses a single iron tank that fed the high-pressure water network in the southern districts. Open to climb on select summer Sundays; visible across all of Favoriten as a marker.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### George-Washington-Hof (Wienerbergstraße 16–20)
A 1.6-km-long inter-war municipal housing complex (1927–30) named for the U.S. president — a deliberate Red Vienna nod to American democracy. Built in pavilion-style with vast open gardens between blocks (1,085 apartments, 50% green space) — the anti-Karl-Marx-Hof, low-rise where Ehn went high.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Quellenplatz Marian column
A 1721 Baroque Marian column transplanted to Quellenplatz from an inner suburb during the 1890s — Mary on a spiral pillar surrounded by four saints (Roch, Sebastian, Charles Borromeo, Rosalia). Sits stranded in a small triangular square in the heart of Anatolian Favoriten, conducting a continuous and silent religious dialogue with the neighborhood.
*Sources: outer_districts*

## District 1110 — Simmering

### Neugebäude (Otmar-Brix-Gasse 1)
A massive 16th-century Renaissance imperial palace ruin on the eastern edge of Simmering — Maximilian II built it 1569–1576 on the spot where the Ottoman sultan's tent stood during the 1529 siege, as a deliberate "we took your camp" gesture. Long arcaded courtyard, ruined twin towers, slowly being restored. The most enormous building in Vienna almost nobody knows exists.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Kaiser-Ebersdorf Schloss (Kaiser-Ebersdorf, Münnichplatz)
A 13th-century moated castle that Maximilian I used as a hunting lodge — Charles V slept here on his way to the Diet of Worms in 1521. Now mostly hidden behind a 19th-century prison wall (still a working prison), but the gatehouse, drawbridge moat-line and one tower are visible from the public street. A medieval castle that became a state penitentiary by historical accident.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Geiselberg watertower & cemetery hill view
The Geiselberg is a small rise on the Simmeringer Heide with a 1910 brick water tower at its summit — climb the public path and you get the only ground-level view of the Zentralfriedhof's entire 2.5 km length, plus the refinery flares of Schwechat on the horizon. The most underrated panorama in Vienna's east.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Simmeringer Heide (Simmeringer Haide)
An unmowed steppe-grassland remnant on Vienna's south-east edge — the last fragment of the pre-urban Pannonian dry grassland that once covered the whole eastern basin. Protected since 1992; in May it's purple with sage and milkvetch. A piece of the Hungarian Puszta inside Vienna city limits.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Alberner Hafen — Friedhof der Namenlosen (Alberner Hafen 230)
A small cemetery on the south Danube bank for unidentified bodies that washed ashore in the harbor eddy — 104 graves marked only with wrought-iron crosses bearing "Unbekannt" (the cataloged ones have a year and a number). Currently the project is to remember them collectively, not their tragedies individually. The chapel and the cobble path through the willows are silent.
*Sources: outer_districts*

## District 1120 — Meidling

### Theresienbad (Hufelandgasse 3)
A Jugendstil public bath (1898, rebuilt 1956 after wartime damage) on the site of a 1750s mineral spring discovered when Maria Theresa was empress — hence the name. The exterior keeps the 1898 turret and tile work; the interior is mid-century modern. Vienna's oldest continuously-operating public swimming pool.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Meidlinger Hauptstraße pedestrian zone
A 600-metre Gründerzeit shopping street pedestrianised in 1989 — Vienna's first outside-the-Ring pedestrian zone. The 1880s tenements still have their stucco angels and shop signs; the southern end terminates at the Bezirksamt with a small Jugendstil clock tower. The "small Mariahilfer Straße" for locals who can't afford it.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Haydnpark (former Hundsturm cemetery)
A small park on the site of the old Hundsturm cemetery — Joseph Haydn was originally buried here in 1809 (his skull was famously stolen by phrenologists; only reunited with the body in 1954 at Eisenstadt). The park keeps a few salvaged Baroque grave markers along its walls — including Haydn's first headstone — as a quiet memorial garden.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Tivoligasse / Edelsitz Schönbrunner Schlossgasse axis
A narrow lane running along the western edge of Schönbrunn Park, lined with Biedermeier "Edelsitze" (noble country seats) from when Meidling was Habsburg dacha country — small palaces with garden walls, wisteria and cracked stucco coats of arms over the gates. Walk it at dusk for the imperial-twilight feel.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Phorus-Park (Phorusplatz)
A pocket park named after the constellation Argo Navis's prow ("Phorus") because the surrounding streets were named in the 1880s after astronomical features — Sternwartepark, Tirolergasse, Aichholzgasse make a 19th-century astronomy district. A 1900 cast-iron bandstand sits at the center.
*Sources: outer_districts*

## District 1140 — Penzing

### Otto-Wagner-Spital pavilions (Baumgartner Höhe)
Around Wagner's golden-domed church (already catalogued) extends a 144-hectare campus of 60 identical pavilions (1904–1907) — Carlo von Boog's masterpiece of "pavilion psychiatry," each ward a free-standing yellow Jugendstil block in a forest setting. Wandering the campus on the public paths is wandering the world's largest preserved Belle Époque hospital landscape.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Jubiläumswarte (Johann-Staud-Straße)
A 31-metre concrete observation tower on the Wilhelminenberg's western slope (1956, replacing an 1898 wooden tower), free to climb. 360-degree panorama: Stephansdom east, the Wienerwald west, the Schneeberg on a clear day. One of two free public viewpoints over Vienna (the other is the Habsburgwarte).
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Hadikgasse 28 — Schönbrunner Brücke villa quarter
Where Hietzinger Hauptstraße becomes Hadikgasse runs a strip of 1880s villas built for Imperial-era civil servants who wanted to walk to Schönbrunn but live cheaper than Hietzing. The houses have wrought-iron garden gates, faded family monograms, and one or two surviving carriage barns. A bourgeois fossil belt.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Technisches Museum exterior (Mariahilfer Straße 212)
The 1909 neo-classical museum building — Emperor Franz Joseph laid the cornerstone for his diamond jubilee. Exterior is freely walk-around: stone reliefs of inventors above the entry (Werner Siemens, Carl Auer von Welsbach, Ressel — the propeller engineer also Vienna-buried), and the Stephanie-Warte original wrought-iron observation tower restored on the grounds.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Hietzinger Brücke — Wienfluss portal
Where the Wien river crosses out of Schönbrunn and dives underground heading east, Otto Wagner's stadtbahn engineering team built a granite-faced portal arch (1898) with Secessionist iron grilles — practically invisible from the bridge above. Stand on the bridge, lean over, and the river vanishes into Vienna's most beautiful storm drain.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Penzinger Friedhof — Hans-Moser grave & cemetery
A small district cemetery (founded 1781) holding Hans Moser, the beloved Viennese character actor whose mumble-grumble dialect entered the city's collective unconscious. The cemetery's chestnut alley and 1860s chapel are publicly accessible. (Cemetery as botanical green-space rather than memorial — Moser's grave is just a hint of its layered identity.)
*Sources: outer_districts*

## District 1150 — Rudolfsheim-Fünfhaus

### Westbahnhof façade (Europaplatz 2)
The 1952 reconstruction of Vienna's Western Railway terminus — a clean post-war modernist face with a continuous concrete canopy and a large bronze relief by Joseph Humplik ("Help in Need"). The 1858 original was destroyed in 1945. Inside the former station hall, the original 1950s mosaic-floor pattern survives.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Reindorfgasse art-street
A narrow 1-km Gründerzeit street that the 2010s turned into Vienna's most concentrated street-art corridor — every other shutter painted, semi-curated by the local "Reindorfgassenfest" community festival. The houses are 1880s-bourgeois plain; the layer on top is contemporary graffiti. The walk happens entirely outdoors.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Märzpark (Mariahilfer Gürtel)
A 5-hectare park named after the March 1848 revolution that briefly toppled Metternich. Carved into the park's edge sits a stone memorial of the 1848 barricades. The park borders the Stadthalle and connects to the Schmelz via a footbridge — a green hinge between western working-class districts.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Storchengasse — Storchenhaus relief
A 19th-century apartment building with a large stork relief over the entry — the bird carrying a swaddled baby in its beak, a 1900s natalist motif. The street is named after the bird, the house quotes the street's name, and the relief explains both: a self-referential Vienna street.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Stadthalle exterior (Vogelweidplatz 14)
Roland Rainer's 1958 multi-purpose hall — Austria's largest indoor arena, a key piece of post-war modernism. The roof is a hyperbolic-paraboloid concrete shell suspended from V-shaped piers; from across the Gürtel it reads as a single tense gesture. Free to walk around any time outside event hours.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Maria-vom-Siege-Kirche (Mariahilfer Gürtel 76)
A vast 1875 neo-Romanesque pilgrimage church with a 76-metre dome — Friedrich von Schmidt (the Rathaus architect) designed it as a memorial to the relief of Vienna in 1683. Stands isolated on a traffic island where Mariahilfer Gürtel meets the Gürtel: the most dramatically situated church in the western districts, almost no tourists visit.
*Sources: outer_districts*

## District 1160 — Ottakring

### Habsburgwarte / Wilhelminenberg-Warte (Wilhelminenberg)
A 23-metre stone observation tower (1888) on the Wilhelminenberg summit, built for Empress Elisabeth's birthday — five storeys of cut stone, free to climb. Panorama from Bratislava to the Schneeberg. The path up runs through the Wilhelminenberg vineyards, with the Schloss Wilhelminenberg (1908) just below.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Yppenmarkt brunch ring
The 1880s market hall and its surrounding cobbled square — Vienna's most diverse food market, the cluster where Yugoslavs, Turks, Austrians and 2010s natural-wine bars all share one set of café tables. Saturday morning is the social peak. Distinct from Brunnenmarkt (north end is Yppenmarkt, south is Brunnenmarkt).
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Ottakringer Brauerei (Ottakringer Platz 1)
Vienna's only big brewery still inside city limits — founded 1837, brick-and-iron Gründerzeit production halls, public brewery yard accessible during opening hours. The 35-metre red brick chimney is an Ottakring skyline marker. Friday afternoon Biergarten opens into the courtyard among the copper kettles.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Sandleitenkapelle (Liebknechtgasse / Sandleitenhof)
A tiny 1928 Red Vienna chapel folded inside the Sandleitenhof complex — a socialist housing project building a worker's chapel was a contested act (the SDAP was militantly secular). The compromise: a stripped-down expressionist brick chapel with no spire, just a cross above the door. Architecturally unique in Vienna.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Kongresspark & Liebknechtgasse statues
A large park between Ottakringer Straße and the Sandleitenhof — created 1928 with a vast central rose garden, a 1930 children's wading pool (still working), and bronze allegorical statues of "Work" and "Family" by Anton Hanak that look more like Soviet-realist propaganda than Red Vienna. Wedding photos every Saturday.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Wilhelminenberg vineyards
Vienna is one of the few capitals with commercial vineyards inside city limits — the Wilhelminenberg slope, west of Ottakring, has been planted since the 12th century. Public footpaths run between the rows; the Heurigen (new-wine taverns) at the top serve the wine pressed 50 metres downhill. A working vineyard you can walk a tram-ride from the inner city.
*Sources: outer_districts*

## District 1170 — Hernals

### Kalvarienberg-Kirche (Kalvarienberggasse 23)
A 1700 pilgrimage church on Hernals's only hill, with 17 outdoor chapels (the *Kreuzweg*) lining the path up the slope — each chapel a small Baroque shrine with painted relief. Used to host the largest Easter pilgrimage in Vienna; now a quiet ascent through a stations-of-the-cross arranged like a sculpture garden. Free.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Hernalser Hauptstraße — Mädchen mit Korb relief
At Hernalser Hauptstraße 78, a high-relief sculpture of a girl with a basket marks an 1880s laundry — the Hernals stream once powered washing operations for the whole western city. A small commercial sign that became sculpture, then survived its trade by a century.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Elterleinplatz fountain
The geographical center of Hernals — a triangular square with a 1909 cast-iron drinking fountain that still works in summer (Vienna's free public-fountain network has ~900 of these). The square is named after a 17th-century mayor; the surrounding façades are pure 1890s Gründerzeit.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Schwarzenbergpark (Neuwaldegg) entrance
The largest English landscape park in Vienna (1765), at Hernals's western edge — owned originally by the Schwarzenberg family, donated to the city 1958. Allées of beech, a Doric "temple of friendship," a serpentine stream. The entry gates on Waldeggstraße are wrought-iron 18th-century survivors.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Heiliger Mathias Kirche & Hernalser Friedhof corner (Veronikagasse)
A 1910 brick parish church by Karl Holey with a 50-metre tower visible across the district. The chestnut-shaded square in front holds a Madonna column (1726) transplanted from the inner city when the church was built — a 200-year time shuffle in one small space.
*Sources: outer_districts*

## District 1200 — Brigittenau

### Wallensteinplatz & Wallensteinstraße market
A long rectangular square with a Saturday farmers' market — named for General Wallenstein of the Thirty Years' War, who had a country house here when Brigittenau was floodplain. The 1898 Brigittakapelle sits at the south end; the 1898 cast-iron Jugendstil drinking fountain at the north end still works. A 1.5 km market promenade ends at the U6 Jägerstraße.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Brigittakapelle (Forsthausgasse)
The chapel for which Brigittenau is named — a 1645 octagonal Baroque pilgrimage chapel to Saint Brigid of Sweden, the only octagonal Baroque chapel in Vienna. Sits in a small park; in summer hosts open-air concerts. Once stood completely alone on Danube floodplain; now ringed by 1900s tenements.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Augarten Porzellanmanufaktur garden gate (Obere Augartenstraße 1)
Inside the Augarten's wall sits the 1705 Baroque palace housing Europe's second-oldest porcelain manufactory (founded 1718) — the corner garden gate (free to enter) opens into a small forecourt with a fountain shaped like a Habsburg porcelain teapot, scaled to architectural size. A self-quoting building.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Nordbahn ghost line (Innstraße / Nordbahnstraße)
The original Vienna-Brno railway (1837, Austria's first steam line) terminated here; the station was bombed in 1945 and never rebuilt. The platform foundations, a section of original wrought-iron canopy, and one signal mast survive as a 19th-century railway ruin in a 21st-century construction site — currently being rewilded as a public park.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Friedrich-Engels-Platz fountain
At the center of the Engelsplatz-Hof complex (already in catalog as architecture) sits a 1933 abstract bronze fountain by Anton Hanak — three nude male figures bearing the basin on their shoulders, called "Aufbau" (Build-up). One of Hanak's last works before he died; pure Red Vienna iconography.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Hannovermarkt (Hannoverplatz)
A 1898 Gründerzeit market square with an iron-and-glass market hall, less famous than Karmelitermarkt but with a stronger Romanian/Bulgarian/Polish flavour from 2000s migration. The Friday spice stalls are the social hub of the immigrant inner-Brigittenau.
*Sources: outer_districts*

## District 1210 — Floridsdorf

### Schlingerhof (Brünner Straße 34–38)
A Red Vienna housing complex (Hans Glaser, 1925–26) with one of the most theatrical entry archways in Vienna — a 15-metre brick gateway under a stepped tower-gable, framing an interior courtyard with sgraffito panels of workers. The Schlingerhof's covered market still runs Tuesday–Saturday; the Hof is named for socialist politician Hans Schlinger.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Floridsdorfer Spitz (Am Spitz)
The triangular square where Floridsdorf's three main streets converge — anchored by the 1898 Floridsdorfer Pfarrkirche and the 1888 cast-iron market pavilion. A surprising piece of Belle Époque urban planning across the Danube; pure Habsburg-suburban texture.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Bruckhaufen Spinnerin am Kreuz cluster (Mengergasse)
A small 18th-century wayside shrine called the "Spinning Madonna" — a stone column with a relief of Mary spinning, marking what used to be the path workers walked between Vienna and the Marchfeld farms. Folk legend says women who touched the column got better at spinning. Tiny, unguarded, easy to miss. (Distinct from the famous Spinnerin am Kreuz in Favoriten, which is gallows-associated and excluded.)
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Wasserpark (Floridsdorfer Wasserpark)
Vienna's only public park built around an active drinking-water reservoir — the 1910 brick Wasserspeicher 2 sits at the center, a Romanesque-revival fortress of pumps, ringed by a swan-and-lily lake. Cycling paths, a Hanak bronze ("Wasserträgerin," 1929), and surprisingly few tourists.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Bruckhaufen island & old Donaumeisterhaus
The small Bruckhaufen quarter — a historic Danube boatmen's settlement that became an island when the Danube was straightened in 1875. Single-story 19th-century fishermen's cottages, one street, ends at the river. The 1830 Donaumeisterhaus (boat-master's house) still stands.
*Sources: outer_districts*

## District 1220 — Donaustadt

### Lobau wilderness gate (Saltenstraße / Esslinger Furt)
The western edge of the Lobau — a 23-km² wilderness floodplain forest, part of Donau-Auen National Park, within the 22nd district. Free entry through a small gate; reed beds, beavers, herons, the silted-up branches of the old Danube. The most genuinely wild place inside any major European city.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Kaisermühlen old village (Schüttauplatz)
Before the UN City and the DC Tower, Kaisermühlen was a Danube fishermen's hamlet — and the village core still survives behind the towers: low cottages, a 19th-century parish church, the "Kaisermühlen-Blues" of Kottan-Detective fame. Walk in from Donauinsel U1 and the towers vanish in two minutes.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### UN City / VIC exterior (Wagramer Straße 5)
Johann Staber's 1979 complex of six interconnected Y-shaped towers — UN's third headquarters, after New York and Geneva, on technically extraterritorial land. The plaza in front (Memorial Plaza) is publicly accessible: a bronze globe sculpture by Henry Moore-school sculptor Fritz Wotruba's circle, and the 1979 Peace Bell donated by Japan rung once a year on UN Day.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Aspern Seestadt (Sonnenallee, Aspern)
Vienna's largest new-town development (begun 2010, completion 2028+) — an entire district of 240 hectares built around a 5-hectare artificial lake, with the U2 line extended to serve it. The plan was developed by Madrid's Tovatt Architects + the city; streets are all named after women (Hannah-Arendt-Platz, Maria-Tusch-Straße). A 21st-century Vienna under construction.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Stadlauer Ostbahnbrücke (Donaustadt south)
The 1870 wrought-iron railway bridge over the Danube — Austria's longest 19th-century iron truss. The east bank has a small pedestrian path under the bridge with the original 1870 plaques in German and Hungarian, marking when the Imperial-Royal Eastern Railway crossed into Habsburg Hungary.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Mühlwasser Auenpark (Donaustadt center)
A linear park along an old Danube side-arm that survived the 1875 river-straightening — willow-shaded, with a 1900s wooden footbridge and a small wetland viewing platform. Beavers reintroduced 2010s, frequently visible at dusk. A pocket of pre-19th-century riverscape three U-Bahn stops from the DC Tower.
*Sources: outer_districts*

## District 1230 — Liesing

### Wotruba-Kirche (Ottillingerplatz 1, Mauer)
Fritz Wotruba's 1976 brutalist concrete church on the Georgenberg — 152 asymmetric concrete blocks stacked into a tetrahedral pile that reads as accidental from a distance and crystalline up close. Wotruba was a leading Austrian sculptor; his church has no nave, no axis, no symmetry — just light entering through the chance gaps. The most radical religious building in Austria.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Maurer Wald (Mauer forest)
A 6-km² beech-oak forest on Vienna's south-western edge, with the Anninger and Föhrenberge hills rising at its far end. Public hiking paths, signed Wanderwege, the "Roter Wanderweg" loop. The forest borders the Wotruba-Kirche; you can hike from the brutalist church into proper woods in five minutes.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Kalksburg Schloss & arboretum (Promenadegasse 3)
The 1745 Schloss Kalksburg — a small Baroque country palace once owned by the Liechtenstein family, now a Jesuit boarding school, with publicly accessible gardens and an arboretum of exotic trees (Lebanese cedar, ginkgo, sequoia). The chapel courtyard has a 1730 Baroque marble fountain.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Atzgersdorf old village core (Breitenfurter Straße)
The historic core of Atzgersdorf — Liesing absorbed it in 1938 — with a small village square, the 1740 St. Catherine parish church, and the "Atzgersdorfer Mühle" (the old miller's house with its working waterwheel preserved). Tram 60 stops at the square. Feels like a Lower Austrian village inside Vienna.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Liesinger Brauerei tower (Brauergasse)
The surviving 1840 brick tower of the former Liesing Brewery — the brewery closed in 1979 but the malthouse tower was preserved as an industrial landmark, with its original wrought-iron weathervane shaped like a hop flower. Now stranded inside a residential development; you can walk right up to the base.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Perchtoldsdorfer Heide (south Liesing edge)
A protected dry-grassland steppe on the boundary with Perchtoldsdorf — orchids, bee-eaters, sand lizards. Like the Simmeringer Heide but warmer and richer; in May covered in yellow flax and pasque-flower. Vienna's south-western nature reserve, ten minutes by tram from a U6 station.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Kaiserin-Elisabeth-Allee (Mauer)
A 1.5-km tree-lined allée Empress Elisabeth used as her private riding path between Hermesvilla and the Mauer hills — 250 plane trees planted 1885, still standing. Now a quiet residential street, but the proportions are imperial: 14 metres wide, dead straight, with old stone milestones every 200 metres.
*Sources: outer_districts*

## District 1130 — Hietzing (supplemental — Lainzer / Hermes additions)

### Lainzer Tor (Hermesstraße / Lainzer Tor)
The principal gateway into the Lainzer Tiergarten — a 1782 Baroque iron-and-stone gatehouse with the imperial double eagle still mounted on the keystone. The wall runs 22 km around the reserve, enclosing it since Ferdinand I in 1561. From outside the gate, the wall just goes on forever in both directions.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Roter Berg viewing tower (St.-Veit-Gasse)
A small 1898 wooden observation tower on the Roter Berg hill — restored 2010, free to climb. Views over Hietzing, Schönbrunn's Gloriette, and the Wienerwald. The hill itself is a former vineyard; old stone wine-press foundations are scattered around the path.
*Sources: outer_districts*

### Hütteldorfer Schloss Wolkersbergen
A 1718 Baroque country house on the Wolkersberg in upper Hietzing — yellow stucco, hipped roof, surrounded by a garden the city now keeps semi-public. The view down into Hütteldorf and across the Wien valley is what Joseph Roth described as the "real countryside ten minutes from the Hofburg."
*Sources: outer_districts*
