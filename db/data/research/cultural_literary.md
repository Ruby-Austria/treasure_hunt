# Vienna Cultural & Literary GPS Catalog

Outdoor-only anchors for cryptic riddle clues — literature, cinema, music (living arts, not death), philosophy, intellectual history. Every entry is free to reach, GPS-friendly, and avoids Holocaust / Nazi-era / death-focused framings. Composers and writers are tied to where they **lived, wrote, played** — not where they died.

Grouped by Vienna district (1010–1230). 40 entries.

---

## District 1 — Innere Stadt (1010)

### 1. Café Central
- **Address:** Herrengasse 14
- **Tied to:** Trotsky, Lenin, Peter Altenberg, Alfred Polgar, Adolf Loos, the entire chess-and-revolution set
- **Hook:** "Herr Bronstein from the Café Central" was Foreign Minister Czernin's brush-off when warned in 1917 that a Russian revolution was imminent. Bronstein became Trotsky. Inside sits a wax Altenberg at "his" table — but the building itself, Palais Ferstel, is the riddle: a Renaissance-revival arcade you walk through like a covered street, and the chess players of 1913 are still notionally seated upstairs.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — arcade is a public passage, façade is on Herrengasse.

### 2. Café Hawelka
- **Address:** Dorotheergasse 6
- **Tied to:** H. C. Artmann, Konrad Bayer, Gerhard Rühm, Helmut Qualtinger, Oskar Werner, André Heller, Nikolaus Harnoncourt
- **Hook:** Survived WWII with not a pane of glass broken. In the 60s/70s every interesting person in Vienna passed through this smoke-stained room — Artmann himself said without it "much would have remained undone, unsaid, or even unthought of." Leopold and Josefine Hawelka ran it themselves until they were nearly a century old.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — narrow Dorotheergasse façade, easy to photograph.

### 3. Café Bräunerhof
- **Address:** Stallburggasse 2 (corner with Bräunerstraße)
- **Tied to:** Thomas Bernhard
- **Hook:** Bernhard sat here daily, reading every newspaper Vienna offered while railing in print against the city that served him coffee. The unrenovated 1970s interior is famously dim and the waiters famously brusque — exactly the "Austrian hostility" Bernhard required as creative weather. On Sundays there is still live salon trio music.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — corner façade visible from Stallburggasse.

### 4. Café Landtmann
- **Address:** Universitätsring 4
- **Tied to:** Sigmund Freud, Gustav Mahler, Marlene Dietrich, Bruno Kreisky, every Austrian chancellor since
- **Hook:** Freud's café of choice — he'd cross Schottentor from Berggasse 19 to read papers here. It is also where Austrian politics still happens: ministers brief journalists at these tables. The terrace faces the Burgtheater across the Ring, giving you a coffee-house view of the empire's most consequential stage.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — Ringstrasse-facing façade and terrace are pavement-level.

### 5. Café Sacher
- **Address:** Philharmonikerstraße 4
- **Tied to:** Operngoers, Habsburg afterglow, the Sachertorte
- **Hook:** Eduard Sacher's wife Anna ran the hotel for decades in a cloud of cigar smoke and French bulldogs, courting archdukes who slipped in through a side door to dine with their actresses. The torte recipe is still litigated. The exterior on Kärntner Straße is small; the legend is large.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — Opera-side façade, no entry required.

### 6. Café Mozart
- **Address:** Albertinaplatz 2
- **Tied to:** Mozart (loosely), Graham Greene, *The Third Man*
- **Hook:** Greene drafted scenes of *The Third Man* sitting here in 1948, and Anton Karas's zither theme is named "The Café Mozart Waltz." Joseph Cotten and Alida Valli's characters meet here in the film. Outside there is no plaque marking this — only the name on the awning.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — Albertinaplatz frontage.

### 7. Café Korb
- **Address:** Brandstätte 9
- **Tied to:** Sigmund Freud, Elfriede Jelinek, Andy Warhol, Arthur Miller
- **Hook:** Opened 1904 with Emperor Franz Joseph at the ribbon. Owner Susanne Widl is herself a 1970s conceptual-art figure — she runs a café where Jelinek (Nobel 2004) drinks her coffee at a designated table while a "Philosophicum" of public philosophy meetings happens downstairs. A Vienna café that is also an ongoing artwork.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — Brandstätte corner just behind Stephansdom.

### 8. Café Prückel
- **Address:** Stubenring 24 (corner Biberstraße)
- **Tied to:** Jugendstil Vienna, Egon Friedell's circle, post-war café redesign by Oswald Haerdtl
- **Hook:** Opened Christmas Day 1903 by a former champion bicycle racer. Haerdtl's 1955 redesign of the front room is itself a protected work of mid-century modern. From 1931 to 1938, cabaret "Der liebe Augustin" ran in the cellar; many of its performers were émigrés who later returned. Sunday afternoon piano salon still runs.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — large windowed corner on the Ring.

### 9. Café Schwarzenberg
- **Address:** Kärntner Ring 17
- **Tied to:** The original Ringstrasse café (1861)
- **Hook:** The first café built into the Ring after the city walls came down — it has watched 165 years of Vienna walk past its windows. Coachmen of the imperial guard once tied horses outside; today the same façade looks across to the Russian war memorial fountain. The least disguised time-capsule on the Ring.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — Ringstrasse façade.

### 10. Mozarthaus / Figarohaus
- **Address:** Domgasse 5
- **Tied to:** Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart
- **Hook:** The only Mozart apartment in Vienna that survives intact. He lived here 1784–1787 — his happiest, richest years — and wrote *Le nozze di Figaro* in this building, plus three of the "Haydn" string quartets. The 1906 plaque on the wall understates this absurdly: there is no fanfare, just a small stone marker on a narrow medieval lane behind the cathedral.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — exterior on Domgasse, free to stand and read the plaque.

### 11. Pasqualatihaus (Beethoven's apartment, 1804–08 & 1810–14)
- **Address:** Mölker Bastei 8
- **Tied to:** Ludwig van Beethoven
- **Hook:** Beethoven rented apartments here on-and-off for 11 years from his patron Baron Pasqualati. He wrote the Fifth and Sixth Symphonies, *Fidelio*, the Archduke Trio, and *Für Elise* under this roof — perched atop a fragment of the medieval city wall (a "Bastei") that the Ringstrasse never demolished. (Note: the *Eroica* itself was composed in Oberdöbling, not here.)
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — exterior is on a raised, public, walled terrace above the street.

### 12. Schreyvogelgasse 8 — the Harry Lime doorway
- **Address:** Schreyvogelgasse 8
- **Tied to:** *The Third Man* (Carol Reed, 1949), Orson Welles
- **Hook:** The doorway where Welles steps into a sliver of streetlight and looks up — the first time anyone in the film sees Harry Lime alive. A cat brushes his shoe. It is a real door on a real lane behind the Burgtheater, with no signage. You stand exactly where the camera stood.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — public lane.

### 13. Maria am Gestade staircase
- **Address:** Salvatorgasse 12 (foot of stairs on Tiefer Graben side)
- **Tied to:** *The Third Man*, *Before Sunrise*, Gothic Vienna
- **Hook:** One of Vienna's oldest Gothic churches, perched on what was literally the medieval river bank. Holly Martins flees down these stairs in *The Third Man*; Jesse and Céline walk past in *Before Sunrise*. Two films, half a century apart, used the same flight of stone as a portal between worlds.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — exterior staircase, public.

### 14. Hotel Sacher (Third Man exterior)
- **Address:** Philharmonikerstraße 4
- **Tied to:** *The Third Man*, British Army HQ 1945–49
- **Hook:** Requisitioned as British officers' quarters after WWII, the Sacher is where Holly Martins is summoned by Major Calloway. Greene's choice was practical and atmospheric: of all imperial-era hotels left standing in 1948, this still had a wine cellar.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — same Kärntner-side façade as the café.

### 15. Casanova Revuebühne
- **Address:** Dorotheergasse 6–8 (immediately adjoining Café Hawelka)
- **Tied to:** *The Third Man*
- **Hook:** In the film, Holly traces Popescu (the slippery Romanian "third man") to this club. The interior in the movie was a studio set, but the actual Casanova on Dorotheergasse is still here, still operating as cabaret, sharing a façade with the bohemian Hawelka. Two worlds — coffee philosophers and cabaret racketeers — sharing one address.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — Dorotheergasse façade.

### 16. Burgkino
- **Address:** Opernring 19
- **Tied to:** *The Third Man* (continuous weekly screenings since the 1950s)
- **Hook:** A standard mid-century Vienna cinema by appearance, but it has shown *The Third Man* in English nearly every week for over seventy years. Tuesday, Friday, Sunday. Cash only. The film never closes.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — Ringstrasse façade, marquee visible from pavement.

### 17. Sigmund Freud Park & bench (Votivkirche side)
- **Address:** Universitätsring opposite Votivkirche
- **Tied to:** Sigmund Freud
- **Hook:** The park is the monument — there is no statue, only a bench and a stone marker. Freud passed it daily walking to and from the University; the city named the grass after him in 1985 precisely as anti-monument. The decision was deliberate: a place to sit, not to gaze upward.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — public park.

### 18. University of Vienna Arkadenhof
- **Address:** Universitätsring 1 (enter from Ring, free access to courtyard)
- **Tied to:** Freud, Boltzmann, Doppler, Schrödinger (later busts), the Vienna Circle by association
- **Hook:** An arcaded Renaissance-revival courtyard ringed by 150+ busts of professors. A free open-air pantheon of Austrian intellectual history. Doppler unveiled 1901, Boltzmann posthumous, Freud added belatedly in 1955 after long resistance. You can pass them all in fifteen minutes — every thinker who shaped modern physics, medicine, philosophy, philology, glaring at one another across a fountain.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — courtyard is open to the public during university hours.

### 19. Vienna Circle plaque — Boltzmanngasse 5 (Institute of Physics)
- **Address:** Boltzmanngasse 5
- **Tied to:** The Wiener Kreis (1924–1936), Schlick, Carnap, Neurath, Hahn, Gödel
- **Hook:** The Thursday-evening seminar of the Vienna Circle met in a ground-floor classroom of this building from 1924 to 1936 — and reinvented philosophy from there. A plaque on the façade marks the room. The street itself is named for Boltzmann, whose entropy formula is engraved on his Arkadenhof bust around the corner.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — façade plaque, public sidewalk.

### 20. Schreyvogelgasse / Mölker Bastei junction
- **Address:** Schreyvogelgasse meets Mölker Bastei
- **Tied to:** Beethoven (Pasqualatihaus) **and** *The Third Man* (Harry Lime doorway is at no. 8)
- **Hook:** A 30-second walk connects the place Beethoven wrote the Fifth Symphony to the doorway where Harry Lime first appears. Two utterly different Viennas, one tiny lane. Riddle gold: a single GPS point can disambiguate between music history and noir cinema.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 21. Grillparzer-Denkmal — Volksgarten
- **Address:** Volksgarten, near the Ringstrasse / Parliament side
- **Tied to:** Franz Grillparzer
- **Hook:** Unveiled 1889. A seated Grillparzer with six marble reliefs around him depicting his plays. Bernhard wrote in *Alte Meister* about hating this monument from a Kunsthistorisches Museum bench across the Ring — the kind of view that defined Viennese literary feuds across half a century.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — public park.

### 22. Goethe-Denkmal (facing Schiller across the Ring)
- **Address:** Opernring / corner Burggarten
- **Tied to:** Goethe
- **Hook:** Bronze Goethe sits massively at the Burggarten edge, unveiled by Franz Joseph himself on 15 December 1900. About 180 metres across the Ring, Schiller stands. The two friends look at each other across six lanes of traffic — a deliberate piece of urban choreography.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 23. Schiller-Denkmal (facing Goethe)
- **Address:** Schillerplatz (in front of the Akademie der bildenden Künste)
- **Tied to:** Friedrich Schiller
- **Hook:** Unveiled 10 November 1876, Schiller's birthday. Four corner statues at his feet represent the ages of life: mother-and-baby, youth, man, old man. Behind him stands the art academy that twice rejected a young applicant named Hitler — a fact the city does not advertise but you can verify from the steps.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 24. Burgtheater — *Heldenplatz* premiere
- **Address:** Universitätsring 2
- **Tied to:** Thomas Bernhard
- **Hook:** Bernhard's *Heldenplatz* premiered here on 4 November 1988 to scheduled scandal — protesters dumped manure at the door, ministers walked out. The play indicts Austria's amnesia by setting it on the actual square outside, where in 1938 the Anschluss was cheered. The façade itself is the scene.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — Ringstrasse-facing exterior.

### 25. Heldenplatz
- **Address:** Heldenplatz (between Hofburg and Ringstrasse)
- **Tied to:** Bernhard (the play), Musil (the parade scenes of *Mann ohne Eigenschaften*)
- **Hook:** Vienna's grand parade-ground. Musil's *Parallelaktion* — the absurd patriotic committee that never decides what to celebrate — is staged in this space. Today: equestrian statues of Prince Eugene and Archduke Charles, two emperors-elect of Habsburg memory, and the empty middle that Bernhard's play makes deafening.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

## District 2 — Leopoldstadt (1020)

### 26. Riesenrad (Wiener Riesenrad)
- **Address:** Riesenradplatz 1 (Prater)
- **Tied to:** *The Third Man*, Graham Greene's "cuckoo clock" speech
- **Hook:** Built 1897. Harry Lime delivers his speech here — looking down at the "dots" that are human lives, justifying his diluted-penicillin racket. You don't need to ride it; standing under the gondola arc you are exactly where Welles improvised the most famous monologue Greene didn't write.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — Prater entrance is free.

### 27. Nestroy-Denkmal
- **Address:** Praterstraße 17 (corner Zirkusgasse, near Nestroyplatz U-Bahn)
- **Tied to:** Johann Nestroy
- **Hook:** Nestroy — Vienna's 19th-century satirist, the man Karl Kraus called Austria's only real dramatist — got his statue in 1929, then it wandered: stored, exiled to Penzing for three decades, finally returned 1983 to within shouting distance of Praterstraße where his Carltheater once stood. A monument that itself has a picaresque biography.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

## District 3 — Landstraße (1030)

### 28. Haus Wittgenstein
- **Address:** Parkgasse 18 (formerly addressed as Kundmanngasse 19), 1030
- **Tied to:** Ludwig Wittgenstein (designed it 1926–28 for his sister Margaret Stonborough)
- **Hook:** Wittgenstein had finished the *Tractatus*, given away his fortune, and tried being a village schoolteacher. Then he spent two years obsessing over door-handle proportions and radiator placement for this severe, almost punitive Modernist house. He wrote later: "Architecture is a gesture." The wrought-iron gate on Parkgasse is the way in; the rusted gate on Kundmanngasse is sealed. The yard is open during Bulgarian Cultural Institute hours, and the exterior is always visible.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — façade and front garden viewable from street.

### 29. Hofmannsthal — Salesianergasse 12
- **Address:** Salesianergasse 12
- **Tied to:** Hugo von Hofmannsthal
- **Hook:** Hofmannsthal's parental apartment, where the teenage prodigy wrote the lyric poetry that astounded all of Vienna under the pen-name "Loris" before anyone realised he was 17. A spacious late-Biedermeier flat — he complained he couldn't concentrate here because of his nervous mother and the maids. He fled to Rodaun.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — façade.

### 30. Karl Kraus / Die Fackel office
- **Address:** Hintere Zollamtsstraße 3
- **Tied to:** Karl Kraus, *Die Fackel* magazine (1899–1936)
- **Hook:** Kraus published 922 issues of *Die Fackel* from this address — much of it written by him alone, often through the night, dictating in red ink to printers in the same building (Jahoda & Siegel). He used the magazine to wage thirty years of one-man war on Austrian journalism, hypocrisy, and language itself. No flashy plaque; just an unremarkable façade that housed the loudest voice in the empire.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

## District 4 — Wieden (1040)

### 31. Brahms apartment marker — Karlsgasse 4
- **Address:** Karlsgasse 4 (the original building was demolished; plaque on its successor)
- **Tied to:** Johannes Brahms
- **Hook:** Brahms lived on the top floor here from late 1871 until his death in 1897 — his entire late period: the Fourth Symphony, the late piano intermezzi, the Clarinet Quintet. The original building is gone but the address is the same and a marker remains. The Karlskirche dome looms behind it; he saw it from his window every morning.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — façade plaque.

## District 5 — Margareten (1050)

### 32. Filmcasino
- **Address:** Margaretenstraße 78
- **Tied to:** Vienna independent cinema
- **Hook:** Founded 1911, the same year as the Bellaria — one of Vienna's two oldest surviving cinemas. The 1950s remodel left an intact post-war foyer that itself looks like a film set. Programmes lean to international auteur cinema; the building is its own art object.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

## District 6 — Mariahilf (1060)

### 33. Café Sperl
- **Address:** Gumpendorfer Straße 11
- **Tied to:** Franz Lehár, the Secession-era artistic crowd; Lehargasse runs along its flank
- **Hook:** Open since 1880 — same Thonet chairs, same marble, same carambole billiard tables. Lehár dropped in regularly while writing operettas at the Theater an der Wien around the corner; you can still play billiards on tables he saw. The side street is literally called Lehargasse.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — corner façade.

### 34. Theater an der Wien
- **Address:** Linke Wienzeile 6
- **Tied to:** Mozart (Magic Flute aftermath), Beethoven (*Fidelio* premiere 1805), Lehár (*Die lustige Witwe* premiere 1905)
- **Hook:** Built 1801 by Emanuel Schikaneder — the very man who wrote the libretto for Mozart's *Zauberflöte* and originated the role of Papageno. Look for the "Papageno gate" on the side façade: Schikaneder cast in stone as the bird-catcher. Beethoven lived briefly in the building above the theatre while finishing *Fidelio*, which premiered here in 1805 to an audience largely of Napoleon's occupying officers. A century later, *The Merry Widow* premiered on the same stage (30 Dec 1905) — Vienna's biggest theatrical export of the 20th century.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — Papageno gate visible from Millöckergasse side.

### 35. Haydn-Haus
- **Address:** Haydngasse 19
- **Tied to:** Joseph Haydn
- **Hook:** Haydn bought this modest suburban house in 1793 and lived here until his death — but the relevant fact is that he wrote *The Creation* (*Die Schöpfung*, 1796–98) and *The Seasons* (1799–1801) at this address. An old man inventing modern oratorio in a quiet two-story bourgeois house on what was then the city's edge. Brahms, who idolised Haydn, walked past this building daily — they were a 10-minute stroll apart.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — modest façade with plaque.

## District 7 — Neubau (1070)

### 36. Bellaria Kino
- **Address:** Museumstraße 3
- **Tied to:** Vienna's golden-age studio cinema, classical Hollywood and German-language film
- **Hook:** Founded 1911. Specialises in classic-era films — Marlene Dietrich, Hans Moser, Romy Schneider seasons. Closed 2019, reopened April 2026 after community pressure. The cinema where Vienna goes to see itself on screen.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

## District 8 — Josefstadt (1080)

### 37. Stefan Zweig apartment — Kochgasse 8
- **Address:** Kochgasse 8
- **Tied to:** Stefan Zweig (1907–1919)
- **Hook:** Zweig lived here for the twelve years that made his name — early *Sternstunden der Menschheit*, the Verlaine and Verhaeren translations, the friendship with Romain Rolland, the war. The 1962 plaque on the façade says only: "This house was from 1907–1919 home and place of work of the poet Stefan Zweig (1881–1942)." Tight, factual, almost defiant — the same prose register as Zweig himself.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — façade plaque.

## District 9 — Alsergrund (1090)

### 38. Berggasse 19
- **Address:** Berggasse 19
- **Tied to:** Sigmund Freud (1891–1938)
- **Hook:** Freud lived and saw patients here for 47 years. *Die Traumdeutung*, the Wolf Man, the Rat Man, Anna O., the founding of psychoanalysis — all from this address. The street door, the staircase, the brass plate are visible from the public pavement without paying a cent. He always preferred entering through the back garden in autumn.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — façade and entrance hall (often) viewable.

### 39. Strudlhofstiege
- **Address:** Strudlhofgasse / Liechtensteinstraße junction
- **Tied to:** Heimito von Doderer
- **Hook:** A 1910 Jugendstil staircase by Theodor Jaeger, with curved steps, ornate green railings, a fish-mouth fountain. Doderer's 1951 novel *Die Strudlhofstiege* turned this stairway into a literary cosmos: 900+ pages of Viennese lives intersecting on these 58 steps. A plaque from 1962 carries his own poem about the stairs — text and place collapse into each other.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — public outdoor staircase.

### 40. Schubert Geburtshaus exterior
- **Address:** Nußdorfer Straße 54
- **Tied to:** Franz Schubert (born here 31 January 1797)
- **Hook:** A modest two-story Biedermeier survivor on a busy commercial street. Schubert's father was the schoolmaster; the family of fourteen children crammed into a "Pawlatschenhaus" arrangement around a courtyard. The plaque is small. The contrast with what came out of this address — six hundred Lieder — is the whole point.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — façade with plaque.

## District 18 — Währing (1180)

### 41. Schnitzlervilla
- **Address:** Sternwartestraße 71
- **Tied to:** Arthur Schnitzler (lived here 1910 until 1931)
- **Hook:** Schnitzler bought this villa in 1909 and wrote his great late novellas here — *Fräulein Else*, *Traumnovelle* (the basis for Kubrick's *Eyes Wide Shut*). The plaque, unveiled on his centenary in 1962, names him simply "der Dichter Arthur Schnitzler." The "Cottage" neighbourhood around it was the Austrian intellectual bourgeoisie's preferred retreat — Mahler, Freud, and Schnitzler within a 15-minute walk.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — villa façade visible from street.

## District 19 — Döbling (1190)

### 42. Eroica-Haus
- **Address:** Döblinger Hauptstraße 92
- **Tied to:** Ludwig van Beethoven, the *Eroica* (Symphony No. 3)
- **Hook:** Beethoven spent the summer of 1803 in Oberdöbling — then an outer village, now part of the 19th district — and on this stretch of Döblinger Hauptstraße he drafted the *Eroica*, the symphony that broke open the 19th century. (Local historians note the city designated this façade in 1970 as the "correct" Beethoven address but his actual house was likely nearby at Hofzeile 15; either way, this lane is where Symphony No. 3 was thought into existence.) Tram 37 stops one block away.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — façade plaque on a quiet residential street.

## Notes on what was deliberately left out

- **Schubert / Mozart / Beethoven death-houses, Zentralfriedhof composer graves, Schlick's assassination spot at the University staircase** — excluded by the "no death sites" constraint.
- **Berggasse 19 inside** — paid museum; only the exterior is in this list.
- **Mendel** — born and worked in Brno, not Vienna; only the small Gregor-Mendel-Platz in the 18th district carries his name, and it is thin material for a clue.
- **Joseph Roth's hotel** — Roth famously *worked from* the Hotel Bristol and Imperial bars, but he is more strongly tied to his deathbed in Paris, so we use the Bristol exterior on Kärntner Ring 1 only as a tangential cue if a clue calls for "the hotel where Roth dictated columns to waiters." Not formally numbered here because the hotel link is mood, not residence.
- **Robert Musil's exact Vienna address** is not reliably documented in accessible sources; his clearest physical anchor in Vienna is Heldenplatz (entry 25) as the imaginative stage of *Mann ohne Eigenschaften*.
