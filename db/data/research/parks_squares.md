# Vienna Parks, Squares, Fountains & Outdoor Rooms — Treasure Hunt Catalog

A curated research file for the RubyConf Austria 2026 treasure hunt (May 29–31). All entries are outdoor, free to physically reach, and GPS-friendly (open sky). Morbid sites (Holocaust, Nazi-era, plague victims, composer-deathbeds, execution sites) are intentionally excluded. The hook column emphasizes the layered, encoded, surprising angle a cryptic clue could lean on.

---

## District 1010 — Innere Stadt (Inner City)

### 1. Volksgarten — Theseustempel
- **Address:** Volksgarten, Dr.-Karl-Renner-Ring, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A miniature Athenian temple in the middle of Vienna — Pietro Nobile's exact recreation of the Temple of Hephaestus, built in the 1820s to house Canova's *Theseus Slaying the Centaur*. The statue moved to the Kunsthistorisches Museum in 1890, so the temple stands empty: a Greek shrine to a hero who is no longer there. Vienna's first public park sits on the bones of a Napoleonic-era demolished bastion (the Burgbastei).
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes. Open park, no entry needed, GPS clear.

### 2. Volksgarten — Rose Garden
- **Address:** Volksgarten, near the Theseustempel
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Around 3,000 rose bushes in about 400 varieties — a botanical archive disguised as decoration. Late May is peak bloom (perfect timing for the conference). The Habsburgs literally turned a defensive earthwork into a perfumed garden — fortification as flower bed.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 3. Volksgarten — Sisi (Empress Elisabeth) Memorial
- **Address:** Volksgarten, parallel to the rose garden, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Tucked away from the main paths sits a marble Sisi, seated, gazing into a reflecting pool surrounded by a long tree-lined avenue — unveiled 1907, nine years after her Geneva assassination. Friedrich Ohmann's architecture, Hans Bitterlich's statue. The pool, fountains, and arcade form an entire ritual "room" most tourists walk past without noticing.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 4. Burggarten — Mozart-Denkmal
- **Address:** Burggarten, Josefsplatz side, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Viktor Tilgner's 1896 monument was originally on Albertinaplatz; it walked to Burggarten in 1953. At Mozart's feet, the city gardeners plant a *living treble clef* in seasonal flowers — a musical score you can only read in summer. The plinth's front relief depicts the young Mozart at the harpsichord with his father and sister.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes. Park is free.

### 5. Burggarten — Palmenhaus exterior + Schmetterlinghaus
- **Address:** Burggarten, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A 180-meter steel-and-glass Jugendstil cathedral, completed 1901–1907 by Friedrich Ohmann. The west wing now houses a tropical butterfly habitat, the east wing a brasserie — the same glass shell shelters Lepidoptera and Aperol Spritz. The building's curve mirrors the bastion that used to stand here.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — viewable from the Burggarten lawn.

### 6. Heldenplatz — Prince Eugene equestrian statue
- **Address:** Heldenplatz, in front of Neue Burg, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Anton Fernkorn's 1865 bronze. Eugene of Savoy was refused a commission by Louis XIV (allegedly too short, too ugly), rode to Vienna in 1683, and became the empire's greatest general. His horse rears on its hind legs — a sculptural feat Fernkorn pulled off only after the Archduke Karl statue, by counterweighting the bronze. The plinth faces *outward*, away from the Hofburg, toward the lands he conquered.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 7. Heldenplatz — Archduke Karl equestrian statue
- **Address:** Heldenplatz, in front of Neue Burg, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Same square, same sculptor (Fernkorn, 1860), but the engineering is more famous: the horse balances on only two hind legs with no external support — at the time, a structural near-miracle. Karl beat Napoleon at Aspern-Essling in 1809 (Napoleon's first land defeat). The pair of statues makes Heldenplatz a sculptural dialogue: Habsburg vs. Bourbon, Ottoman vs. Napoleonic.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 8. Maria-Theresien-Platz — Triton & Mermaid Fountains
- **Address:** Between Kunsthistorisches and Naturhistorisches Museums, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Four matching Carrara-marble fountains (1894) flank the Empress's monument: Tritons, mermaids, children on rocks, shells and swans. Most visitors look up at Maria Theresa's bronze throne and never count that there are *four* identical water mirrors arranged geometrically around her — a Habsburg compass rose.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 9. Maria-Theresien-Platz — Maria Theresa Monument
- **Address:** Center of Maria-Theresien-Platz, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** 19 meters tall, by Kaspar von Zumbusch (unveiled 1888). In her left hand she holds the Pragmatic Sanction — the legal document that made her, a woman, eligible to inherit the Habsburg lands. Four equestrian generals at the base, fourteen advisors around the plinth — including Mozart's teacher Gluck and the young Haydn. A whole court in bronze.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 10. Michaelerplatz — Roman Ruins (Hans Hollein excavation)
- **Address:** Michaelerplatz, in front of Hofburg's Michaelertor, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A sunken oval cut into the cobbles reveals Roman Vindobona — houses from the 2nd–5th century AD, part of the *canabae* (civilian quarter outside the legion fort). Hans Hollein laid out the dig in 1991. Above it, the dome of the Hofburg; across the square, Adolf Loos's stripped-down Looshaus (1912) that scandalized Emperor Franz Joseph. Four time-layers in one open-air room.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — open square.

### 11. Am Hof — Mariensäule
- **Address:** Am Hof, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Built 1647, recast in bronze 1667. The Virgin spears a dragon at the top; at the base, four winged Putti slay a snake, a basilisk, a lion, and a second dragon — *heresy, plague, war, famine*. A whole apocalypse in cherub form. The square itself sits on Roman barracks foundations and was the Babenberg ducal palace in the 12th century.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 12. Am Hof Square
- **Address:** Am Hof, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** One of Vienna's largest cobbled squares, layered like an archaeological core: Roman garrison → Babenberg castle (1156) → market → Jesuit church (Kirche am Hof, where the end of the Holy Roman Empire was proclaimed in 1806). The pavement itself is the artifact.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 13. Freyung — Austriabrunnen
- **Address:** Freyung square, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Ludwig Schwanthaler's 1846 fountain. Five allegorical figures: at the top, Austria; at her feet, four reclining river-gods — Elbe, Vistula, Danube, Po — the watershed of the old empire compressed into one basin. The bronze was cast in Munich and shipped down the Danube; legend says smugglers tried to use the hollow figures to hide contraband cigars during transit.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 14. Hoher Markt — Vermählungsbrunnen
- **Address:** Hoher Markt, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Vienna's oldest square (Roman forum site), with a 1732 fountain by Johann Emanuel Fischer von Erlach. The "Marriage Fountain" looks like it celebrates Habsburg dynastic union — but the figures are actually *Mary and Joseph at their wedding before a high priest*, an event recorded in no Bible and rarely depicted in art. A cryptic religious tableau hiding in plain sight on a market square.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 15. Hoher Markt — Ankeruhr
- **Address:** Hoher Markt 10, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Franz von Matsch's 1914 art clock. Above: the sun, flanked by a child with a butterfly (life) and an hourglass (time). Below: a basilisk (the legendary Viennese basilisk of nearby Schönlaterngasse). Twelve figures parade across at noon — Marcus Aurelius, Charlemagne, Maria Theresia, Haydn… The last figure was supposed to be Haydn playing the Imperial Anthem, but Germany kept the melody for its own anthem after 1922, so the parade plays a different Haydn tune.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — viewable from the square.

### 16. Stephansplatz — Stock im Eisen
- **Address:** Corner of Graben and Kärntner Straße at Palais Equitable, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A medieval tree trunk bristling with hammered iron nails, encased behind glass on a corner of the square. Legend: every passing apprentice locksmith driving a nail in for luck. The earliest documented nail dates to 1533. Behind the glass is one of the only surviving "nail trees" in Europe — a folk database of anonymous travelers, encoded in iron.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — outdoor corner display.

### 17. Stephansplatz — Virgilkapelle outline
- **Address:** Stephansplatz, above the U-Bahn entrance, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Outlined in lighter paving stones on the square is the floor-plan of the Virgilkapelle — a 13th-century underground chapel rediscovered in 1973 during U-Bahn construction, 12 meters below the cobbles. Walk over it and you're standing on a hidden medieval room.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — surface marker.

### 18. Kohlmarkt — Hofburg dome sightline
- **Address:** Kohlmarkt, Michaelerplatz end, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Kohlmarkt = "coal market," a memory of when wood and charcoal vendors worked the gate of Roman Vindobona here. Stand at the Michaelerplatz end and the entire street frames the green Michaelertor dome — a deliberate Baroque axis. Demel's confectionery at #14 served both Franz Joseph and Sisi; the rivalry with Sacher over the original torte is unresolved.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — pedestrian street.

### 19. Graben — open pedestrian "room"
- **Address:** Graben, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** "Graben" literally means *trench* — this elegant baroque street is the filled-in Roman defensive ditch outside Vindobona. The whole golden mile is a buried fortification. Two facing fountains (Josefsbrunnen and Leopoldsbrunnen, 1804) bracket the street like exclamation marks.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 20. Franziskanerplatz — Mosesbrunnen
- **Address:** Franziskanerplatz, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A tiny L-shaped square hidden behind the Franciscan church, almost invisible from any main thoroughfare. The 1798 Moses Fountain by Johann Martin Fischer shows Moses striking the rock; the front relief shows the Israelites drinking. Next door is Kleines Café — said to be the smallest coffeehouse in Vienna. A pocket square that feels like a film set.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 21. Altes Rathaus — Andromedabrunnen courtyard
- **Address:** Wipplingerstraße 8, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** The last work of Georg Raphael Donner (1741), in the courtyard of the Old City Hall. Lead relief, not bronze — Donner was famous for using soft, malleable lead to render Andromeda chained to her rock and Perseus rescuing her. The Habsburgs once forced Donner to recast a nude *Providentia* (now in the Belvedere) because the church found it indecent — this Andromeda is similarly bold.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — public courtyard, freely walkable.

### 22. Rathauspark
- **Address:** Around Rathaus, between Stadiongasse and Felderstraße, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Two symmetric halves north and south of the Rathaus, each with its own fountain, vast old trees, and meandering paths. The "park inside the Ring" most overlooked because the Rathaus building itself overshadows it. In May, lilacs and chestnut blossoms anchor the experience.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 23. Pallas-Athene-Brunnen (Parliament)
- **Address:** Dr.-Karl-Renner-Ring 3, in front of Parliament, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Theophil Hansen designed it (1898–1902): Athena in gilded helmet, holding Nike (Victory) in her right palm. At her feet recline four river gods — Danube, Inn, Elbe, Vltava — the four arteries of the old empire. Local joke: Athena turns her back on Parliament because she's disgusted by what happens inside. Symbol of wisdom watching legislation, judgment in bronze.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 24. Augustinerbastei — Erzherzog Albrecht equestrian
- **Address:** Augustinerbastei, terrace beside Albertina, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** One of the only surviving bastions of Vienna's demolished city walls — a ten-meter-high terrace right beside the Albertina. Caspar von Zumbusch's 1899 bronze of Archduke Albrecht (victor at Custoza, 1866) sits on a Czech-granite plinth. From this terrace you can see the State Opera, the Albertinaplatz, and a cross-section of the *layer-cake* of imperial Vienna.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — open terrace, free to walk.

### 25. Mölker Bastei — Beethoven's bastion
- **Address:** Mölker Bastei 8, corner Schreyvogelgasse, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Another bastion-stump from the 16th-century city wall, ramping up from the modern street. The Pasqualatihaus sits on top — Beethoven lived here on and off from 1804–1815 and wrote the 5th and 6th Symphonies, *Für Elise*, *Fidelio*, and the Archduke Trio in these rooms. The exterior brick wall of the bastion is still visible from the street; you can touch a piece of Vienna's destroyed fortification.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — exterior is public.

## District 1020 — Leopoldstadt

### 26. Augarten — Baroque parterre
- **Address:** Obere Augartenstraße / Hauptallee, 1020
- **District:** 1020
- **Hook:** Vienna's *oldest baroque garden*, opened to the public by Joseph II in 1775 — and free ever since. The park is shaped like a kite. Inside: the imperial porcelain manufactory (founded 1718, second-oldest in Europe). Even now, you can wander the cubist-clipped chestnut and lime alleys at no cost.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 27. Karmelitermarkt + Karmeliterplatz
- **Address:** Karmeliterplatz / Im Werd, 1020
- **District:** 1020
- **Hook:** Smaller and quieter than Naschmarkt; revived in the 2010s as a creative-class neighborhood market. Fewer stalls, more atmosphere, with cafés spilling out into the square next to the 17th-century Carmelite church. Saturday mornings are the moment.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 28. Prater Hauptallee — chestnut avenue
- **Address:** Praterstern to Lusthaus, 1020
- **District:** 1020
- **Hook:** A 4.5-km arrow-straight allée lined with ~2,500 chestnut trees. Joseph II opened the Prater to the public in 1766 — an Enlightenment gesture so radical that the nobility protested for losing their private hunting grounds. In late May the chestnuts are in full bloom, the white candles glowing over the path.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 29. Prater — Lusthaus
- **Address:** Freudenau 254, end of Hauptallee, 1020
- **District:** 1020
- **Hook:** An octagonal Habsburg pleasure pavilion at the far end of the Prater Hauptallee — originally an imperial hunting outpost rebuilt in 1781–1783 by Isidore Canevale. It marks the geographical *vanishing point* of the avenue: walk 4.5 km straight, and you arrive at this small yellow octagon in a clearing.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — exterior fully accessible.

## District 1030 — Landstraße

### 30. Belvedere Gardens (free zone) — Sphinxes & Kaskadenbrunnen
- **Address:** Prinz-Eugen-Straße 27, 1030
- **District:** 1030
- **Hook:** The entire baroque garden between Upper and Lower Belvedere is *free*. Sphinxes — women's heads on lion bodies, symbols of strength + intelligence — flank the upper terrace. Twelve statues representing the months line the grand staircase. The Kaskadenbrunnen tumbles down five tiers. Dominique Girard laid it out 1700–1725.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 31. Schwarzenbergplatz — Hochstrahlbrunnen
- **Address:** Schwarzenbergplatz, 1030 (south end)
- **District:** 1030
- **Hook:** Built 1873 to celebrate the First Vienna Spring Water Pipeline from the Alps. The fountain is a *calendar in water*: 365 small jets (days), 12 high jets (months), 24 jets (hours), 6 mid-jets and a central island (weeks), 30 jets (days of a month). The water itself comes from the Hochschwab massif, 100+ km away. Illuminated in colors after dark.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 32. Stadtpark — Johann Strauss "gilded violinist"
- **Address:** Stadtpark, near Kursalon, 1030/1010 border
- **District:** 1030
- **Hook:** Edmund Hellmer's 1921 bronze, gold-plated on the outside. The gold leaf was stripped off in 1935 (Depression-era austerity) and re-gilded only in 1991 — for 56 years the "Golden Strauss" was just dull bronze. Behind him, a marble arch with sculpted naiads in flowing dance, frozen in mid-waltz.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 33. Stadtpark — Donauweibchen-Brunnen
- **Address:** Stadtpark, northwest section, 1030/1010 border
- **District:** 1030
- **Hook:** Hanns Gasser's 1862 Carrara-marble "Danube Maiden" — a river nymph who, in folklore, warned fishermen of coming floods. Originally destined for the Fischhof fish market, the statue sat in a city depot for two years before landing in the Stadtpark in 1865. The 1948 stone copy (after WWII damage) is what stands today.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 34. Stadtpark — Wienflussportal (Ohmann Jugendstil)
- **Address:** Stadtpark, where the Wienfluss enters open air, 1030 side
- **District:** 1030
- **Hook:** Friedrich Ohmann and Josef Hackhofer's 1903–1906 Jugendstil masterpiece: a curved hemicycle of staircases, balustrades, and twin domed pavilions where the underground Wienfluss surfaces. The original plan called for water-spouting bronze elephants and a veil-waterfall over the keystone — both cut for budget. The bones of an unfinished Art Nouveau dream remain.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

## District 1040 — Wieden

### 35. Karlsplatz — Resselpark & Henry Moore "Hill Arches"
- **Address:** Resselpark, in front of Karlskirche, 1040
- **District:** 1040
- **Hook:** A pond holds Henry Moore's *Hill Arches* (1973), donated to the city in 1978. The whole eastern park is a sculpture garden punctuated by Johannes Brahms (seated, brooding) and Siegfried Marcus (automotive pioneer). The reflecting pool doubles the Karlskirche dome — a visual trick set up by post-war urban planners.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 36. Karlsplatz — Otto Wagner Stadtbahn Pavillons
- **Address:** Karlsplatz, 1040
- **District:** 1040
- **Hook:** Two mirrored Jugendstil train pavilions (1898–1899), apple-green steel and white marble, golden sunflower motifs. Originally Stadtbahn stations, they were moved here in 1960. The east one is a café; the west one is a Wien Museum branch. Wagner's manifesto in three dimensions, free to circle outside.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 37. Naschmarkt
- **Address:** Wienzeile, between Karlsplatz and Kettenbrückengasse, 1040/1060
- **District:** 1040
- **Hook:** "The belly of Vienna." ~130 stalls and eateries built over the now-buried Wienfluss. The Saturday flea market is a cult event. The market roof you walk past is actually the lid over the river. Halfway down the western Wienzeile, two Otto Wagner buildings face each other across the market: the Majolikahaus (1898, all flowered tiles) and the Medaillonhaus (gold medallions by Koloman Moser).
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

## District 1060 — Mariahilf

### 38. Esterházypark
- **Address:** Fritz-Grünbaum-Platz / Gumpendorfer Straße, 1060
- **District:** 1060
- **Hook:** A small green pocket in dense Mariahilf, formerly the garden of the Esterházy palace — the noble family who patronized Haydn for three decades at Eisenstadt. The park itself is a quiet rectangle of plane trees and benches; the Haus des Meeres aquarium sits on its corner. Locals know it as a hidden city living-room.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 39. Mariahilfer Straße pedestrian zone
- **Address:** Mariahilfer Straße, between Westbahnhof and the Museumsquartier, 1060/1070
- **District:** 1060/1070
- **Hook:** Vienna's longest commercial promenade, pedestrianized since 2015 after a fierce political referendum. The eastward sight-line ends precisely at Maria-Theresien-Platz — a 19th-century axis aimed at the empress's throne. A street that argues, in stone, that retail is a civic ritual.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

## District 1070 — Neubau

### 40. Spittelberg quarter
- **Address:** Spittelberggasse / Schrankgasse / Gutenberggasse area, 1070
- **District:** 1070
- **Hook:** A grid of cobbled lanes between MuseumsQuartier and Burggasse, lined with pastel Biedermeier houses (1820s–1840s) painted in faded yellow, pink, and green. Vienna's second-largest pedestrian zone. Small "town-square" pockets emerge unexpectedly between the rows. Once a red-light district, now a craft-and-gallery village.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

## District 1080 — Josefstadt

### 41. Palais Auersperg gardens (exterior)
- **Address:** Auerspergstraße 1, 1080
- **District:** 1080
- **Hook:** Baroque palace where the 7-year-old Mozart played his first Vienna concert (1762). The forecourt and side gardens are accessible from the street — a quiet pocket between the busy ring and the Josefstadt theaters. Often missed because it doesn't look like a public space.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — exterior grounds freely walkable.

## District 1090 — Alsergrund

### 42. Sigmund-Freud-Park
- **Address:** Between Schottentor / Universität and Votivkirche, 1090
- **District:** 1090
- **Hook:** A long green carpet stretching from the University of Vienna's neo-Renaissance pile to the twin neo-Gothic spires of the Votivkirche. Named after Freud (his Berggasse 19 apartment is two blocks north). Big-tree shade, student crowd, chess tables. Not a single Freud bust — just a name and an implication.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 43. Liechtensteinpark (Gartenpalais Liechtenstein)
- **Address:** Fürstengasse 1, 1090
- **District:** 1090
- **Hook:** A baroque private park opened to the public daily 7:15–20:30. Once one of Vienna's three great baroque gardens (with Belvedere and Augarten); now a quiet, almost-forgotten green rectangle in the Rossau. The Palais inside houses the Princely Collections — Rubens, Rembrandt, Raphael — visible only by booking. The park itself is free.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes during stated hours.

### 44. Servitenplatz
- **Address:** Servitengasse / Grünentorgasse, 1090
- **District:** 1090
- **Hook:** Vienna's "Little Paris": a paved square in front of the 17th-century baroque Servitenkirche, ringed by florists, French bistros, and one of the city's quietest pre-Reformation churches. The fountain in the middle, the cobbled platz, and the village feel sit improbably ten minutes from the Ring.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

## District 1130 — Hietzing

### 45. Schönbrunn — Neptunbrunnen
- **Address:** Schönbrunn park, base of the Gloriette hill, 1130
- **District:** 1130
- **Hook:** Franz Anton von Zauner's 1780 fountain. The scene: the sea-goddess Thetis kneels before Neptune, asking safe voyage for her son Achilles to Troy. Maria Theresia commissioned it. The pool *frames* the long axial walk back to the palace — a deliberate Baroque optical trick.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — entry to the park is free.

### 46. Schönbrunn — Roman Ruins (faux Carthage)
- **Address:** Schönbrunn park, east side, 1130
- **District:** 1130
- **Hook:** Built 1778 by court architect Johann Ferdinand Hetzendorf von Hohenberg — fake ruins, deliberately crumbling, as a piece of Enlightenment landscape philosophy: a meditation on the transience of empires. Real Roman spolia were embedded in the artificial walls to make it more "authentic."
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 47. Schönbrunn — Obelisk Fountain
- **Address:** Schönbrunn park, eastern axis, 1130
- **District:** 1130
- **Hook:** A 1777 stone obelisk with fake hieroglyphs — the symbols are pure ornament, not real Egyptian writing. Sits on a grotto of river gods. Egyptomania pre-dating Napoleon's expedition by twenty years.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 48. Schönbrunn — Gloriette hilltop
- **Address:** Gloriette hill, Schönbrunn park, 1130
- **District:** 1130
- **Hook:** Built 1775 to celebrate the Habsburg victory at Kolín (1757). The climb up the hill is free (only the rooftop terrace requires a ticket). From the path beneath the Gloriette arches you get the postcard view of the entire palace + Vienna skyline. A monumental "victory shed" placed at the visual climax of the park axis.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — outdoor walk, free.

## District 1140 — Penzing

### 49. Lainzer Tiergarten — wild boars
- **Address:** Hermesstraße / various gates, 1130/1140
- **District:** 1130/1140
- **Hook:** 24.5 km² walled hunting reserve enclosed by Ferdinand I in 1561 — opened to the public only in 1919. Today: 800–1,000 wild boars roam free, plus mouflons, fallow deer, and red deer. Free entry. Seven gates with different schedules. The Hermesvilla (a Habsburg country retreat built for Sisi) sits at the center of the forest.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes (March–November mainly; some gates year-round).

## District 1180 — Währing

### 50. Türkenschanzpark — Paulinenwarte tower
- **Address:** Türkenschanzpark, 1180
- **District:** 1180
- **Hook:** A 31-meter sandstone observation tower in the middle of the park (1888), named after Princess Pauline Metternich, who championed the park's creation. The "Türkenschanze" itself refers to the 1683 Ottoman siege earthworks that once stood here — the park *is* a former battlefield, now buried under labeled trees from China, Japan, and North America.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes — the tower exterior is always reachable; interior recently reopened.

### 51. Türkenschanzpark — waterfalls & Yunus Emre Fountain
- **Address:** Türkenschanzpark, 1180
- **District:** 1180
- **Hook:** Over 6,500 m² of water surfaces: cascading streams, ponds, a small waterfall. The Yunus Emre Fountain (donated by the Turkish embassy in 1991) honors the 13th-century Sufi mystic-poet — a peaceful gesture set on top of an old Ottoman battlefield. Botanical labels everywhere make the park a free open-air encyclopedia.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

## District 1190 — Döbling

### 52. Setagayapark
- **Address:** Hohe Warte / Gallmeyergasse, 1190
- **District:** 1190
- **Hook:** A pure Japanese garden designed in 1992 by Ken Nakajima, modeled on Kyoto's Shugakuin Palace garden. Bamboo Tokusabari gate, Yukimi-style stone lantern, tea house, koi pond, cascade. Twinned with Tokyo's Setagaya ward. Open March 1–November 30 only. In May, the maples and azaleas peak.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes during stated months.

### 53. Wertheimsteinpark
- **Address:** Döblinger Hauptstraße 96, 1190
- **District:** 1190
- **Hook:** Once a private garden of textile baron Friedrich von Arthaber (1835), passed to the Wertheimstein family in 1867, donated to the city in 1908 with the legal requirement it remain a public park forever. A small pond is the remnant of the Krottenbach stream now culverted underground. The "Blind Garden" (1959), designed for scent and texture, is one of Europe's earliest sensory gardens.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 54. Pötzleinsdorfer Schlosspark
- **Address:** Geymüllergasse / Pötzleinsdorfer Straße, 1180/1190 border
- **District:** 1180
- **Hook:** A landscape park north of Türkenschanzpark with old-growth oaks, a baroque schloss, fenced deer enclosure, hilltop meadows looking toward the Wienerwald. Vienna's classic "neighborhood-but-feels-rural" park — locals walk dogs here, tourists never find it.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

## District 1220 — Donaustadt

### 55. Donaupark — Iris Lake
- **Address:** Wagramer Straße, 1220
- **District:** 1220
- **Hook:** A 25,000 m² artificial lake in the center of the 63-hectare park, ecologically retro-fitted in 1993 to function as a natural water-treatment biotope: the marshy banks filter the water. Above the park stands the 252-meter Donauturm. The open-air stage hosts free concerts May–September.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

### 56. Donauinsel
- **Address:** 21-km island between Donau and Neue Donau, 1210/1220
- **District:** 1210/1220
- **Hook:** A 21-km man-made island engineered 1972–1988 as flood control — accidentally becoming Europe's longest urban beach. Reachable from U1 Donauinsel. Grill zones, water playgrounds, FKK areas (clothing-optional, by law), the legendary Sunken City bar district at the south end. Pure horizontal recreation.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

## District 1160 — Ottakring

### 57. Brunnenmarkt + Yppenplatz
- **Address:** Brunnengasse, leading to Yppenplatz, 1160
- **District:** 1160
- **Hook:** Vienna's longest street market — 170+ stalls of Turkish spice, Balkan börek, Austrian root vegetables. Cheaper, louder, less touristed than Naschmarkt. At the north end, Yppenplatz opens up — a brunch-and-natural-wine plaza where the local creative class spent the 2010s gentrifying. The market lane ends in a literal square. Saturday is the day.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

## District 1010 / Donaukanal — Linear outdoor "room"

### 58. Donaukanal — Schwedenplatz to Urania street-art mile
- **Address:** Donaukanal, between Schwedenplatz and Urania, 1010/1020
- **District:** 1010/1020
- **Hook:** A 1-km legal graffiti gallery — both embankments painted, repainted, layered, over-tagged. Vienna officially legalized certain wall-sections in 1994; the rest is unsanctioned but tolerated. Works change weekly. The Otto-Wagner-designed canal locks at Schützenhaus (Schützenhaus, Obere Donaustraße 26) are the architectural anchor.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Yes.

## Notes for clue authors

- **May 29–31 timing:** Peak rose bloom (Volksgarten), chestnut bloom (Prater Hauptallee), full Türkenschanzpark waterfall flow, maples bright in Setagayapark. Daylight runs ~05:20–20:50.
