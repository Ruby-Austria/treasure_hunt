# Vienna Statues, Monuments & Public Sculpture — Treasure Hunt Catalog

Curated for RubyConf Austria 2026 (May 29–31). All entries are outdoor, free, GPS-reachable, and skew toward the **layered / aesthetic / quirky / celebratory** — avoiding Holocaust, Nazi-era, plague-victim, war-dead, executions, and composer death-bed memorials.

Grouped by district (Bezirk). Each entry: name, address, sculptor/year, the angle for a riddle, and an outdoor-GPS confirmation.

---

## District 1010 — Innere Stadt (the dense core)

### 1. Mozart-Denkmal (Mozart Monument)
- **Where:** Burggarten, near Opernring
- **Sculptor / Year:** Viktor Tilgner (sculptor) + Karl König (architect), unveiled 1896 at Albrechtsplatz; relocated to Burggarten in 1953
- **Hook:** Tilgner literally died five days before the unveiling — his signature on the pedestal had to be finished with his death date. The front relief shows two scenes from *Don Giovanni*, the rear shows the child Mozart playing with his father and sister (after the famous Carmontelle watercolour). The bed of flowers in front is replanted each year into the shape of a **treble clef** — that's the layered clue. Putti on the base personify the *power* of Mozart's music in Art-Nouveau idiom. The pedestal also carries Magic Flute imagery — a discreet wink to Mozart's Freemasonry.
- **GPS:** Open garden, sky visible — yes.

### 2. Johann-Strauss-Denkmal (the gilded waltz king)
- **Where:** Stadtpark (eastern Ringstrasse edge)
- **Sculptor / Year:** Edmund Hellmer, unveiled 21 June 1921
- **Hook:** Looks like solid gold — it's gold-plated bronze. Strauss plays violin under a marble arch crawling with carved nymphs. Allegedly Vienna's most-photographed monument. The unveiling soundtrack was *The Blue Danube*, played by the Vienna Philharmonic. Built by the "Johann Strauss Memorial Committee" founded in 1904; WWI delayed it 17 years.
- **GPS:** Open park, sky-friendly — yes.

### 3. Schubert-Denkmal
- **Where:** Stadtpark (north end)
- **Sculptor / Year:** Carl Kundmann, unveiled 15 May 1872; pedestal by Theophil Hansen
- **Hook:** Carrara marble. The composer's likeness was overseen by his friend, the painter Moritz von Schwind, so this is Schubert as someone who actually knew him remembered him. The reliefs personify "Imagination" (front), "Instrumental Music" (left), "Vocal Music" (right). Funded by the Vienna Men's Choral Society over a decade of charity concerts.
- **GPS:** Open park — yes.

### 4. Anton-Bruckner-Büste
- **Where:** Stadtpark (Schubert end)
- **Sculptor / Year:** Viktor Tilgner, 1891–99
- **Hook:** Tilgner died before Bruckner — meaning a third party finished the bust. Vandals smashed the original muse-of-music base; the current pedestal is a 1988 replacement by Stefan Kameitsky. The riddle layer: the sculptor of Mozart's monument predeceased the composer he was sculpting *here* too — a Stadtpark pattern.
- **GPS:** Open park — yes.

### 5. Franz-Lehár-Denkmal
- **Where:** Stadtpark (south, near the Stadtparkbrücke)
- **Sculptor / Year:** Franz Coufal, unveiled 29 April 1980
- **Hook:** The odd one out in the Stadtpark composer collection — every other monument is 19th-century historicist, but Lehár gets a **Cubist marble** triangle stack behind his stern bust. *Merry Widow* energy in stone. Unveiled with Austrian president Kirchschläger present.
- **GPS:** Open park — yes.

### 6. Donauweibchenbrunnen (Danube Mermaid)
- **Where:** Stadtpark (along the Wienfluss canal)
- **Sculptor / Year:** Hanns Gasser, sculpted 1862, installed 1865
- **Hook:** Viennese mythology's friendly mermaid — she warned fishermen of storms and tossed treasure their way. The folds of her topless drapery hold two fish (referencing the city's professional fishery once based at the Fischhof). Severely damaged in WWII; the current stone copy is by sculptor Fellinger (1948); the original lives in Wien Museum. Four head-mask gargoyles spout into round basins.
- **GPS:** Open park — yes.

### 7. Befreiung der Quelle ("Liberation of the Spring") Fountain
- **Where:** Stadtpark, west bank of the Wienfluss canal
- **Sculptor / Year:** Josef Heu, 1903; ensemble by architect Joseph Urban, opened 16 Nov 1903
- **Hook:** Two nude males, only half-emerging from the rock, strain to lift the boulder blocking the spring — a wildly Symbolist piece for a city park. Reads like Michelangelo's *Prisoners* fused with Art Nouveau. Joseph Urban later moved to America and designed for Ziegfeld and the Met.
- **GPS:** Open park — yes.

### 8. Goethe-Denkmal
- **Where:** Opernring / corner with Goethegasse, facing Burggarten
- **Sculptor / Year:** Edmund Hellmer (yes — same as Strauss), unveiled by Emperor Franz Joseph on 15 December 1900
- **Hook:** A languid, seated Goethe in his fifties stares straight across the Ringstrasse at the Schiller monument ~180 m away. Vienna's longest-running staring contest. The Vienna Goethe Association paid for it.
- **GPS:** Sidewalk on Ringstrasse — yes.

### 9. Schiller-Denkmal
- **Where:** Schillerplatz (in front of the Akademie der bildenden Künste)
- **Sculptor / Year:** Johannes Schilling, unveiled 10 November 1876 (the poet's birthday)
- **Hook:** Schiller stands tall while Goethe sits — different postures, locked gazes. The square appeared in the late 1870s as the old fortification glacis was redeveloped. Surrounded by four allegorical figures representing the four ages of life.
- **GPS:** Open square — yes.

### 10. Maria-Theresia-Denkmal
- **Where:** Maria-Theresien-Platz (between Kunsthistorisches & Naturhistorisches Museum)
- **Sculptor / Year:** Caspar von Zumbusch (sculptor) + Carl von Hasenauer (architect), unveiled 1888 after **14 years** of construction
- **Hook:** The empress sits on her throne holding scepter and the *Pragmatic Sanction* (the legal document that let her inherit at all). At her four corners: allegories of **Justice, Strength, Clemency, Wisdom**. Four equestrian generals at mid-level. The plinth's four sides honour her advisors, administration, military, and arts & sciences. On the arts-and-sciences side: her doctor van Swieten is the central figure, but behind him stand **Gluck and Haydn — and Haydn's hand rests on the shoulder of a young boy who is Mozart**. A riddle goldmine.
- **GPS:** Vast open plaza — yes.

### 11. Pallas-Athene-Brunnen (Parliament fountain)
- **Where:** Dr.-Karl-Renner-Ring 3, in front of Parliament
- **Sculptor / Year:** Carl Kundmann (Athena), Josef Tautenhayn, Hugo Haerdtl; built 1893–1902 on plans by Theophil von Hansen
- **Hook:** 5.5-metre Athena holds a **tiny Nike** (Victory) in her right hand and a spear in her left. The column's capital has **four owls** — wisdom literally supporting wisdom. Originally the planners wanted an Austria-allegory, but the multi-ethnic empire needed something neutral, so a Greek goddess won. River allegories at her feet personify the Danube, Inn, Elbe, and Vltava — the empire's four great rivers.
- **GPS:** Wide-open Ringstrasse — yes.

### 12. Theseustempel (with empty pedestal)
- **Where:** Volksgarten, center
- **Sculptor / Year:** Building by Peter von Nobile, 1819–1823; built to house Canova's *Theseus Slaying the Centaur* (1804–1819)
- **Hook:** This is a 1:1 replica of the **Temple of Hephaestus** in Athens — Vienna's most literal piece of Greek-revival. Canova's marble Theseus was commissioned by **Napoleon** for Milan, then bought by Emperor Franz I and installed here. The statue moved to the KHM staircase in 1890; the temple now hosts contemporary art rotations. The temple is the riddle anchor — Theseus still belongs to it.
- **GPS:** Open lawns — yes.

### 13. Grillparzer-Denkmal
- **Where:** Volksgarten, west side
- **Sculptor / Year:** Carl Kundmann (statue) + Rudolf Weyr (reliefs), 1889
- **Hook:** Lasa-marble seated Grillparzer flanked by six reliefs from his plays — *Sappho*, *Medea*, *König Ottokar*, *Der Traum ein Leben*, etc. He sits in a neoclassical exedra niche like a Roman senator. The Volksgarten's literary heart.
- **GPS:** Open park — yes.

### 14. Empress Elisabeth (Sisi) Memorial
- **Where:** Volksgarten, east end
- **Sculptor / Year:** Hans Bitterlich (figure) + architect Friedrich Ohmann, unveiled 4 June 1907
- **Hook:** **The last imperial monument erected in Vienna.** Sisi sits in white Laas marble — not as empress, but as a private woman: a book, roses, two dogs at her feet. Only her first name on the inscription. Frame this not as memorial-to-the-assassinated but as the private Sisi, the literary recluse who preferred dogs and Greek poetry.
- **GPS:** Open park — yes.

### 15. Joseph II Equestrian Statue
- **Where:** Josefsplatz (Hofburg courtyard between the National Library and the Spanish Riding School)
- **Sculptor / Year:** Franz Anton von Zauner, 1795–1807
- **Hook:** Twelve years to make. Modelled directly on **Marcus Aurelius on the Capitoline** in Rome — Joseph II shown as a Roman conqueror in toga and laurel wreath. Pure Habsburg cosplay: they claimed Roman-imperial descent. Zauner was knighted for it.
- **GPS:** Open courtyard, sky-visible — yes.

### 16. Prinz Eugen Equestrian Statue
- **Where:** Heldenplatz (south side)
- **Sculptor / Year:** Anton Dominik Fernkorn, 1862–65
- **Hook:** The horse rears, supported partly by its tail — a structural cheat. Fernkorn's earlier Archduke Karl across the square achieved a **two-point support** (first ever in monumental bronze). Trying to outdo himself with the Eugen statue **broke him mentally**: he spent the rest of his life in an asylum. Two horses, two riders, two engineering miracles, one ruined sculptor. Frame as celebration of Baroque-era hero (Eugen built the Belvedere).
- **GPS:** Vast open square — yes.

### 17. Erzherzog Karl Equestrian Statue
- **Where:** Heldenplatz (north side)
- **Sculptor / Year:** Anton Dominik Fernkorn, 1853–59, unveiled 22 May 1860
- **Hook:** The **two-point support** miracle: the horse stands rearing on hind legs only, with no hidden brace. First monumental bronze in the world to do this. Karl is depicted at the moment of seizing a standard at Aspern-Essling. Fernkorn was ennobled for the feat. Karl's twin sits opposite — they're a matched pair.
- **GPS:** Vast open square — yes.

### 18. Erzherzog Albrecht Equestrian Statue
- **Where:** Albrechtsrampe, on the Augustinerbastei in front of the Albertina
- **Sculptor / Year:** Caspar von Zumbusch, unveiled 1899
- **Hook:** 7,000 kg of bronze on the highest pedestal-bastion in central Vienna — Albrecht in field-marshal's uniform holding his baton, looking down over the Staatsoper. The Albertina palace is named after him. The statue stands literally **on a bastion of the old city walls** that survived demolition because it held up the museum.
- **GPS:** Open bastion terrace, sky-open — yes.

### 19. Schwarzenberg Equestrian Monument
- **Where:** Schwarzenbergplatz (north end)
- **Sculptor / Year:** Ernst Hähnel, unveiled October 1867
- **Hook:** Commissioned by Franz Joseph in 1861 to honour the man who **co-led the coalition that beat Napoleon at Leipzig in 1813** and marched into Paris. Frame as celebration of the end-of-empire-Napoleon story — the closing chapter of the Napoleonic Wars in stone.
- **GPS:** Wide square — yes.

### 20. Radetzky-Denkmal
- **Where:** Stubenring, in front of the Ministry of Defence (Regierungsgebäude)
- **Sculptor / Year:** Caspar von Zumbusch, 1886–1892; pedestal by Georg Niemann
- **Hook:** 6,000 kg of bronze. Originally on Am Hof, moved to Stubenring in 1912. At the original unveiling, Strauss Sr.'s **Radetzky March** was played — the only military monument with a built-in soundtrack. Reliefs show Radetzky receiving acclaim from soldiers of every branch of the multi-ethnic army.
- **GPS:** Open Ringstrasse — yes.

### 21. Brahms-Denkmal
- **Where:** Resselpark / Karlsplatz
- **Sculptor / Year:** Rudolf Weyr, unveiled 7 May 1908
- **Hook:** Weyr **beat Max Klinger** in an international competition for the commission. Brahms sits enthroned in marble; below him, the muse **Euterpe** (specifically the muse of music) plucks a lyre in despair. The statue's exact location is symbolic: it sits **between Brahms's last apartment** (no longer standing) **and the Musikverein** where he worked the last decade of his life — he walked past this spot daily.
- **GPS:** Open park — yes.

### 22. Beethoven-Denkmal
- **Where:** Beethovenplatz (just off Lothringerstrasse, near the Konzerthaus)
- **Sculptor / Year:** Caspar von Zumbusch, unveiled 1 May 1880
- **Hook:** Beethoven sits like Michelangelo's Moses on a high plinth. Allegorical figures flank him: **Prometheus** (the suffering Titan — Beethoven's deafness equated with Prometheus's eternal torture) and **Nike** (eternal glory). Putti below personify the symphonies. Frame as life/work celebration, not death. 19th-century critics called the Prometheus "the embodiment of restless human longing."
- **GPS:** Open square — yes.

### 23. Donnerbrunnen (Providentia Fountain)
- **Where:** Neuer Markt (also called Mehlmarkt historically)
- **Sculptor / Year:** Georg Raphael Donner, completed 1739 (the originals are in Wien Museum; what's outside is a 19th-c. bronze copy)
- **Hook:** **Providentia**, Roman goddess of foresight, stands center; around the basin four allegorical rivers — Traun, Enns, Ybbs, March. The nude figures **scandalised Maria Theresa**, who in 1773 ordered them melted down for being indecent; luckily they were preserved and returned in 1801. Possibly the first Viennese public art to ditch religious/Habsburg iconography. Frame as the "naked figures that shocked the Habsburgs."
- **GPS:** Open market square — yes.

### 24. Stock im Eisen
- **Where:** Corner of Graben and Kärntner Strasse, at Stephansplatz, embedded in the Palais Equitable façade behind glass
- **Sculptor / Year:** None — it's a tree felled ~1440, ironbound 1575, accidentally-a-sculpture
- **Hook:** A medieval spruce trunk into which travelling smiths and locksmith apprentices hammered nails for luck — the oldest preserved **Nagelbaum** ("nail tree") in central Europe. Legend says the Devil himself locked it shut: the iron padlock is unopenable because the lock's insides are gone. Another legend says a locksmith's apprentice stole a nail to forge an unbreakable padlock to win his master's daughter. The bands are dated 1575 with initials "HB" (Hans Buettinger, the house's owner). Pure Vienna-occult.
- **GPS:** Outdoor street corner; visible from sidewalk through glass — yes.

### 25. Lessing-Denkmal
- **Where:** Judenplatz (south end of the square)
- **Sculptor / Year:** Siegfried Charoux, original 1935, current bronze 1962–65 (re-installed at Judenplatz 1981)
- **Hook:** A tortured timeline — Charoux beat 81 other sculptors in the 1930 competition. The Nazis melted the first version down for weapons in 1939. Charoux remade it after the war. Lessing chosen for *Nathan der Weise* — the play arguing all three Abrahamic religions are equally valid. Frame as the **monument that came back from being melted into bullets** — a survival/return story, not a trauma story.
- **GPS:** Open square — yes.

### 26. Gutenberg-Denkmal
- **Where:** Lugeck (small square just off Rotenturmstrasse, near Stephansdom)
- **Sculptor / Year:** Hans Bitterlich (statue) + Max Fabiani (plinth), unveiled 1900
- **Hook:** Floppy-capped Gutenberg, one hand on hip, bearded and patient. The plinth bears the Latin motto **POST NUBILA PHOEBUS** — "after the clouds, the sun" — i.e. the printing press as enlightenment after medieval darkness. Bitterlich also did the Sisi memorial; Fabiani was Otto Wagner's right hand. Bitterlich took 2.5 years to model it.
- **GPS:** Open small square — yes.

### 27. Otto Wagner Stadtbahn Pavilions
- **Where:** Karlsplatz (both pavilions flank the Resselpark)
- **Sculptor / Year:** Otto Wagner (architecture) + Koloman Moser (gold ornament) + Othmar Schimkowitz (sculpted heads), 1898
- **Hook:** Sunflower patterns in gold leaf on green-and-white marble; the iconic Vienna Secession sunburst motif. The roofline "**Crying Women**" heads are by Schimkowitz — they look like Klimt mosaics rendered in stone. Moser's gold was meant to make the proletariat's commute beautiful: democratic Art Nouveau.
- **GPS:** Open square — yes; sculptural details readable from outside.

### 28. Karlskirche Twin Columns (Trajan-style)
- **Where:** Karlsplatz, southern end
- **Sculptor / Year:** Fischer von Erlach (church), columns wrapped 1716–1737
- **Hook:** Two 47-metre columns flank the church, each wrapped in a **spiral relief** narrating the life of St. Charles Borromeo. They directly quote **Trajan's Column in Rome**, but also reference **Boaz and Jachin** — the two columns at the Temple of Jerusalem (Masonic/Solomonic iconography). Golden **imperial eagles** crown them — this was Habsburg dynastic theatre as much as religion. Frame as architecture-as-monument: a sculptural ensemble visible 360° from open Karlsplatz.
- **GPS:** Wide-open Karlsplatz — yes.

### 29. Rathausmann (Iron Knight atop City Hall)
- **Where:** Rathaus, on the central spire (Friedrich-Schmidt-Platz); a 1:1 replica stands in the Rathauspark
- **Sculptor / Year:** Designed by Franz Gastell, smithed by Alexander Nehr, installed 1882
- **Hook:** A 5.4-metre iron standard-bearer perched 98 metres above the city, cast from **melted-down Russian kopeks** (war reparations). His armour copies Emperor Maximilian I's. He weighs 650 kg and is balanced by an **800 kg iron pendulum-counterweight** hidden in the spire so he can withstand storms. Shoe size: **63 (UK 29)**. The replica in the park lets you stand at his feet and see him at ground level. Pure kitsch-heroic.
- **GPS:** Park replica is fully outdoor; rooftop original visible from anywhere on Rathausplatz.

## District 1020 — Leopoldstadt

### 30. Tegetthoff-Denkmal
- **Where:** Praterstern (the chaotic transit roundabout at the Prater entrance)
- **Sculptor / Year:** Carl Kundmann (sculpture) + Karl von Hasenauer (architect), unveiled 21 September 1886
- **Hook:** **A naval victory monument in landlocked Vienna.** An 11-metre marble column crowned by a 3.5-metre bronze admiral, the shaft studded with **six bronze ram-bows** of warships — directly quoting Roman *columna rostrata*. Modelled after Place Vendôme and Trafalgar Square. Celebrates the 1866 Battle of Lissa over the Italian navy. Frame as "Austria's brief moment as a naval power" — surreal, since the empire today has no sea.
- **GPS:** Wide-open transport hub — yes.

### 31. Nestroy-Denkmal
- **Where:** Nestroyplatz / corner Praterstrasse 17 & Zirkusgasse
- **Sculptor / Year:** Oskar Thiede, unveiled 1929
- **Hook:** Vienna's satirical playwright **as the character Blasius Rohr** from his farce *Glück, Missbrauch und Rückkehr*. A monument-on-walkabout: hidden in the Max-Reinhardt-Seminar courtyard for 30+ years, then moved back near its original spot in 1983. Nestroy was the Mark Twain of Biedermeier Vienna — a wisecrack made bronze.
- **GPS:** Open street corner / small square — yes.

## District 1030 — Landstrasse

### 32. Hochstrahlbrunnen (High-Jet Fountain)
- **Where:** Schwarzenbergplatz (south end)
- **Sculptor / Year:** Engineer Anton Gabrielli, opened 24 October 1873 by Franz Joseph
- **Hook:** Built in **four months** to celebrate the completion of the First Vienna Mountain Spring Pipeline (the Alpine water that still flows from Vienna taps today). The fountain is a literal calendar in water: **365 small jets** (days), **12 high jets** (months), **24 jets** (hours), **6 medium jets** (weekdays + Sunday), and **30 jets** (days in a month). Lit since 1906; now in color. Runs roughly late-March to late-October.
- **GPS:** Open square — yes. (Note: it stands diagonally across from the Soviet War Memorial — avoid framing that.)

## District 1090 — Alsergrund

### 33. Sigmund Freud Memorial Stone
- **Where:** Sigmund-Freud-Park, in front of the Votivkirche
- **Sculptor / Year:** Unveiled by Mayor Helmut Zilk, 6 May 1985 (Freud's birthday)
- **Hook:** A modest stone tablet — not a figural bust outside. Inscribed with the Greek letters **Ψ Α** (Psi-Alpha — Freud's own shorthand for *psychoanalysis*) and his quote *"Die Stimme des Intellekts ist leise"* — "The voice of the intellect is a soft one." Frame as the scientist who invented an entire conceptual vocabulary getting a monument that is itself a notation. (The park also contains a small Vivaldi monument — bonus clue material.)
- **GPS:** Open park in front of Votivkirche — yes.

## District 1100 — Favoriten

### 34. Spinnerin am Kreuz
- **Where:** Triester Strasse / Wienerberg (the western ridge of the 10th district)
- **Sculptor / Year:** Master builder Hans Puchsbaum, 1451–52 (replacing a 1296+ cross destroyed by Hungarian forces in 1446)
- **Hook:** A **medieval Gothic sandstone column** marking what used to be the southern boundary of Vienna's municipal jurisdiction — i.e. the medieval city limits. The legend: a newlywed woman whose husband left on Crusade waited here daily, **spinning wool** to earn money to upgrade the wayside cross into stone. (The user-avoid note: yes, the spot also historically held a gallows — but the **monument itself is the spinning-wife legend**, not the gallows. Frame it as the medieval Penelope-of-Vienna and you're clean.)
- **GPS:** Open street median, fully outdoor — yes.

## District 1130 — Hietzing

### 35. Hermesvilla & Hermes Statue
- **Where:** Lainzer Tiergarten, Hermesvilla grounds (no cars or bikes; ~1 km walk from Lainzer Tor)
- **Sculptor / Year:** Villa by Carl von Hasenauer, 1882–1886; the Hermes garden statue gives the villa its name
- **Hook:** Franz Joseph commissioned this as a "Palace of Dreams" to keep the perpetually-restless **Sisi** in Vienna. The villa's name comes from the statue of **Hermes** (messenger god, traveler, restlessness incarnate — basically Sisi's patron saint) in the garden. Gustav Klimt and Franz Matsch decorated the interiors. The Ulrike Truger "Elisabeth" sculpture (2006) stands in front too. Frame as: she got an entire forested nature reserve as a private retreat and still wouldn't stay.
- **GPS:** Open garden inside the reserve — yes (note: long walk required, and Tiergarten has fixed opening hours).

## Notes for riddle-writing

- **Cross-monument patterns worth exploiting:**
  - Caspar von Zumbusch sculpted Maria Theresia, Beethoven, Radetzky, *and* Albrecht — a "follow the sculptor" multi-stop hunt is possible.
  - Carl Kundmann did Schubert, Grillparzer, Pallas Athene, and Tegetthoff — same.
  - Edmund Hellmer did both Strauss and Goethe.
  - Three Stadtpark composer monuments had sculptors who died before the work was finalized (Mozart's Tilgner, Bruckner's Tilgner, Strauss delayed by WWI).
- **Goethe ↔ Schiller** are a built-in two-stop pair (180 m apart, staring at each other).
- **Prinz Eugen ↔ Erzherzog Karl** are a built-in two-stop pair (Heldenplatz mirror).
- **Karlsplatz cluster** is a five-monument density bomb: Brahms, Karlskirche columns, Otto Wagner pavilions (×2), Hochstrahlbrunnen — all within 300 m.
- **Stadtpark cluster** is the densest: Strauss, Schubert, Bruckner, Lehár, Donauweibchen, Liberation of the Spring — six entries inside one park.
- Donnerbrunnen + Stock im Eisen + Gutenberg are all in the Stephansplatz/Graben pedestrian core — walkable in 10 min.
