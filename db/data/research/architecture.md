# Vienna Architecture Catalog — RubyConf Austria 2026 Treasure Hunt

Outdoor-only, GPS-friendly architectural landmarks in Vienna, curated for cryptic riddle clues. No paid interiors required. No death/Holocaust/plague themes. Grouped by district (1010–1230).

---

## District 1010 — Innere Stadt

### 1. Looshaus (Goldman & Salatsch Building)
- **Address:** Michaelerplatz 3, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Architect / year:** Adolf Loos, 1909–1912
- **Hook:** The building Emperor Franz Joseph reputedly refused to look at — he ordered the curtains of the Hofburg facing it kept closed. Loos's "house without eyebrows" stripped ornament from the upper stories so completely that the city halted construction; he had to bolt on flower boxes as a peace offering, and those bronze planters are still there. The lower floors are clad in green Cipollino marble from Euboea; the Tuscan columns deliberately echo St. Michael's portico across the square. A clue could lean on "the building emperors refused to see" or "the house that grew window boxes under duress."
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Open square (Michaelerplatz). GPS works. Façade fully visible without entry.

### 2. Loos American Bar (Kärntner Bar)
- **Address:** Kärntner Durchgang 10, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Architect / year:** Adolf Loos, 1908
- **Hook:** A tiny street-passage bar whose façade is a stained-glass American flag mounted above four blocks of marble — Loos had lived in the US for three years and was obsessed with American directness. The exterior is essentially a sign and a portal, no more. Hidden alley off Kärntner Straße makes it a satisfying "find me" clue: the flag of one continent embedded in the heart of another.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public alley. GPS works in the passage. Façade visible without entering.

### 3. Secession Building ("Golden Cabbage")
- **Address:** Friedrichstraße 12, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Architect / year:** Joseph Maria Olbrich, 1898
- **Hook:** Topped by a dome of 2,500 gilded iron laurel leaves — locals immediately nicknamed it the "Golden Cabbage" (Goldenes Krauthappel). Above the door: "Der Zeit ihre Kunst, der Kunst ihre Freiheit" ("To every age its art, to art its freedom"). The cube-and-sphere geometry was a rebuke to the Ringstraße's historicism happening one block away. The laurel is for victory, the owls flanking the door for Athena — a temple of art that pretends to be a mausoleum.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public street. Dome and façade fully external. GPS clean.

### 4. Otto Wagner Postsparkasse (Postal Savings Bank)
- **Address:** Georg-Coch-Platz 2, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Architect / year:** Otto Wagner, 1904–1906 (ext. 1912)
- **Hook:** The first modernist office building in continental Europe. Wagner clad the façade in thin marble panels held on by ~17,000 aluminium bolts — the bolts are not structural, they are the ornament. Aluminium was a luxury metal in 1906 (more expensive than silver a generation before). On the parapet stand two aluminium angels by Othmar Schimkowitz. A clue can play on "the building whose nails became its only jewelry."
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Façade on public square. GPS works. Exterior is the whole point.

### 5. Ankerhaus
- **Address:** Graben 10, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Architect / year:** Otto Wagner, 1894–1895
- **Hook:** Wagner invented the multi-use commercial block here — shops with a two-storey glass curtain wall, offices, apartments, and a glass rooftop "photo atelier" perched like a greenhouse on top. Hundertwasser worked in that rooftop studio from 1971 until his death; one architect's prototype became another architect's nest. A vertical sandwich of Vienna modernism.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** On the Graben pedestrian street. GPS clean. Rooftop atelier visible from across the street.

### 6. Anker Clock (Ankeruhr)
- **Address:** Hoher Markt 10–11, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Architect / year:** Franz Matsch (painter/sculptor), 1911–1914
- **Hook:** A Jugendstil bridge clock spanning two halves of the Anker Insurance HQ over Vienna's oldest square (a Roman castrum site). Twelve historical figures parade across in 12 hours; at noon all twelve appear in sequence with period music. The clock is itself a calendar of Vienna's self-image — Marcus Aurelius leads, Haydn ends. Cryptic potential: "where Marcus Aurelius marches past Haydn every noon."
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public square. Visible from below. GPS clean.

### 7. Zacherlhaus
- **Address:** Brandstätte 6 / Wildpretmarkt, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Architect / year:** Jože Plečnik (Wagner pupil), 1903–1905
- **Hook:** Plečnik's only major Vienna work before he left for Prague and Ljubljana. Vertical polished grey granite slabs hung as non-load-bearing cladding — a technique that predates and arguably teaches Wagner's own Postsparkasse façade. An Archangel Michael by Ferdinand Andri stands on the corner; muscular atlantes by Franz Metzner crouch under the eaves. The building where Wagner's student out-Wagnered Wagner first.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Open corner. Façade fully visible. GPS works.

### 8. Engel-Apotheke (White Angel Pharmacy)
- **Address:** Bognergasse 9, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Architect / year:** Oskar Laske (Wagner pupil), 1901–1902
- **Hook:** A pharmacy whose sign is a Jugendstil mosaic of two angels carrying glowing vessels — one of the only Art Nouveau pharmacy façades surviving in Vienna. The "Zum weißen Engel" name dates to 1587; the current building rebuilt the apothecary's medieval identity in gold-leaf and ceramic at the precise moment Vienna invented modern advertising. The angel motif is the brand mark turned into architecture.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public street. Mosaic visible from sidewalk. GPS clean.

### 9. Haas Haus
- **Address:** Stephansplatz 12, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Architect / year:** Hans Hollein, 1985–1990
- **Hook:** Postmodern mirrored drum facing Stephansdom — the most controversial new building of 1990s Vienna. Its convex glass façade was designed to reflect the cathedral back at itself, but the bulbous Ottoman-flavored corner tower above caused riots in the architecture press. A building that exists chiefly to mirror the building across from it. Clue play: "the cathedral's twin in glass" or "what reflects the Gothic at noon."
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Open square. Façade entirely external. GPS clean.

### 10. Rooftop Remodeling Falkestraße
- **Address:** Falkestraße 6, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Architect / year:** Coop Himmelb(l)au, 1983–1988
- **Hook:** Officially "the world's first realized deconstructivist building" — a metal-and-glass creature that looks like it crashed through the roof of a 19th-century lawyer's office. Coop Himmelb(l)au showed the maquette to the mayor, who said it looked "more like art than architecture"; they replied "thank you" because art isn't bound by building code, and built it. Shown at MoMA's 1988 Deconstructivism exhibition. Visible from across the Stadtpark side.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Best viewed from Falkestraße / Stubenring intersection. GPS clean.

### 11. Pallas-Athene-Brunnen (Parliament Fountain)
- **Address:** Dr.-Karl-Renner-Ring 3, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Architect / year:** Theophil Hansen (design 1870), sculptures Carl Kundmann, completed 1902
- **Hook:** A 5.5-meter Athena holding Nike on her palm in front of a Greek-revival parliament — placed there because the Habsburg empire wanted to claim Athenian democratic descent it had never practiced. Her column's capital has four owls (wisdom); the basin below has the four great rivers of the empire as allegorical figures (Danube as woman, Inn as bearded man, Elbe and Vltava). It's a goddess of wisdom holding a goddess of victory in front of a building that had neither.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Open boulevard. GPS clean.

### 12. Hochstrahlbrunnen
- **Address:** Schwarzenbergplatz, 1010 (border 1030)
- **District:** 1010
- **Architect / year:** Anton Gabrielli (engineer), 1873
- **Hook:** A fountain that is a calendar. 365 small jets for days, 12 high jets for months, 24 medium jets for hours, 6 mid-size for weeks, 30 representing days-of-month. Built to celebrate the First Vienna Mountain Spring Pipeline that finally brought Alpine water to the city (1873). It runs only late March to late October — the "winter half" of the year, it sleeps. Clue: "the timepiece that runs on water and only half the year."
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public square. GPS clean. Active during conference dates (late May).

### 13. Palais Daun-Kinsky
- **Address:** Freyung 4, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Architect / year:** Johann Lukas von Hildebrandt, 1713–1719
- **Hook:** Built for Count Daun, who at various points was Viceroy of Naples and Governor of Milan; his portrait gallery once held more conquests than ancestors. By Hildebrandt — same architect as Belvedere — but compact, urban, with one of the most theatrical Baroque staircases in the city (visible from the open courtyard through the gateway). Now an auction house. The palais whose name absorbed a countess.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Façade and courtyard accessible from Freyung. GPS clean. No entry required for façade reading.

### 14. Palais Ferstel
- **Address:** Strauchgasse 4 / Herrengasse 14, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Architect / year:** Heinrich von Ferstel, 1856–1860
- **Hook:** Highly unusual for a Viennese palais to bear its architect's name — Ferstel kept the credit because he had just returned from Italy and grafted Venetian-trecento and Florentine motifs onto what was originally the Austrian National Bank and stock exchange. Houses Café Central, where Trotsky, Freud, Zweig, and Kokoschka argued for decades. The "Donaunixenbrunnen" (Danube mermaid fountain) sits in its glass-roofed passage.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Herrengasse façade fully external. Public passage open. GPS clean.

### 15. Ringturm
- **Address:** Schottenring 30, 1010
- **District:** 1010
- **Architect / year:** Erich Boltenstern, 1953–1955
- **Hook:** Postwar Vienna's first high-rise — 73m, second-tallest on the Ringstraße after Stephansdom. On top is a 20m "weather beacon" with 117 colored lamps that signal tomorrow's forecast by blinking patterns; almost no one in Vienna remembers how to read them. Since 2006 the tower is annually wrapped in a 4,000m² artwork by a different artist from a country where Vienna Insurance Group operates. A building whose skin changes once a year and whose hat predicts the weather.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Visible from Donaukanal and Schottenring. GPS clean.

### 16. Karlsplatz Stadtbahn Pavilions
- **Address:** Karlsplatz, 1010 (border 1040)
- **District:** 1010
- **Architect / year:** Otto Wagner, 1898–1899
- **Hook:** Two tiny apple-green-and-white pavilions facing each other across the square, decorated with gold sunflowers — the surface-level remnant of Vienna's old Stadtbahn (the city railway Wagner designed from end to end). They sit at angles to nothing in particular because the rail tracks beneath them moved. One is a café, the other an exhibition space, but both are pure Jugendstil sugar-cubes that look like they could fold up and be put in a pocket.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Open square. GPS clean. Façades visible 24/7.

## District 1020 — Leopoldstadt

### 17. Uniqa Tower
- **Address:** Untere Donaustraße 21, 1020
- **District:** 1020
- **Architect / year:** Heinz Neumann (HNP), 2001–2004
- **Hook:** A 75m office tower whose floor plan is the letter "Q" (the company is UNIQA). The entire curved façade is a 7,000m² LED media wall with ~160,000 individual LEDs — Vienna's biggest screen, programmed with light-art by Mader/Stublic/Wiermann that morphs nightly. By day a banal glass slab; by night a slow-motion abstract painting along the Donaukanal.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Best view from across the Donaukanal (1010 side). GPS clean.

## District 1030 — Landstraße

### 18. Hundertwasserhaus
- **Address:** Kegelgasse 34–38 / Löwengasse 41–43, 1030
- **District:** 1030
- **Architect / year:** Friedensreich Hundertwasser (with Josef Krawina, Peter Pelikan), 1983–1985
- **Hook:** Municipal social housing dressed as a Klimt painting. 52 apartments, no straight lines, undulating floors ("an uneven floor is a melody to the feet"), trees rooted in apartments that send branches out the windows as "tree tenants." Hundertwasser refused payment for the design, said he was just preventing "something ugly being built." Tiled mosaics by craftsmen who improvised every panel.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public street corner. GPS clean. Interior is private apartments — façade is the whole show.

### 19. KunstHaus Wien
- **Address:** Untere Weißgerberstraße 13, 1030
- **District:** 1030
- **Architect / year:** Friedensreich Hundertwasser, 1989–1991
- **Hook:** Hundertwasser converted the former Thonet bentwood-furniture factory into a museum of himself. Chequerboard façade in black, white, and his beloved tile-greens; uneven cobblestone floor under the entrance arcade. The lesser-known Hundertwasser, much quieter than the famous house ten minutes away — and importantly, the only place in Vienna with his permanent gallery (exterior visible without ticket).
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Façade on public street. GPS clean.

### 20. Rabenhof
- **Address:** Rabengasse 1–3 / Hainburger Straße, 1030
- **District:** 1030
- **Architect / year:** Heinrich Schmid & Hermann Aichinger, 1925–1928
- **Hook:** A Red Vienna "people's palace" with 1,109 apartments around interlocking courtyards — expressionist brick, jagged setbacks, archways like medieval gatehouses. Built with a kindergarten, library, dental clinic, and shared laundry. The complex also houses Rabenhof Theater, a still-active stage. Less famous than Karl-Marx-Hof but architecturally weirder — almost cubist where Karl-Marx-Hof is monumental.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public courtyards open during day. GPS clean.

## District 1040 — Wieden

### 21. Karlskirche
- **Address:** Karlsplatz 10, 1040
- **District:** 1040
- **Architect / year:** Johann Bernhard Fischer von Erlach (begun 1716), completed by son 1737
- **Hook:** A Baroque church flanked by two 47m bas-relief columns directly modeled on Trajan's Column in Rome — depicting the life of St. Charles Borromeo in spiral narrative. The portico is a Greek temple, the dome is Roman, the columns are Imperial — a single building making the explicit claim that Habsburg Vienna is the legitimate fourth Rome. Reflects in the modern pool laid in front of it (Resselpark) for symmetry. The building is essentially a thesis statement.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public park and pool. GPS clean. Exterior is the whole rhetorical move.

### 22. Majolikahaus
- **Address:** Linke Wienzeile 40, 1040
- **District:** 1040 (border 1060)
- **Architect / year:** Otto Wagner, 1898–1899
- **Hook:** Apartment block whose entire façade is glazed ceramic tile painted with floral motifs that flow upward like vines climbing the building — invented because Wagner wanted a façade that could be hosed clean (a sanitary obsession of the period). The tile is "Majolika," industrial ceramic. Next door, Linke Wienzeile 38 is its sister building with golden Koloman Moser muse-medallions instead of flowers; both face the Naschmarkt.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Façade on busy public street. GPS clean.

### 23. Linke Wienzeile 38 (Musenhaus / Medallion House)
- **Address:** Linke Wienzeile 38, 1040
- **District:** 1040 (border 1060)
- **Architect / year:** Otto Wagner, 1898; ornament by Koloman Moser
- **Hook:** Wagner's other Wienzeile house — same year, same block as Majolikahaus, but instead of tile, Moser's gold-leaf medallions of stylized muses peer down from beneath palm-frond stucco. A roof-line crown of stylized female heads in copper. Many visitors mistake the two for one building; they are a deliberate pair, one tiled, one gilded.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Façade on public street. GPS clean.

## District 1060 — Mariahilf

### 24. Köstlergasse 3 (Wagner's own apartment)
- **Address:** Köstlergasse 3, 1060
- **District:** 1060
- **Architect / year:** Otto Wagner, 1898–1899
- **Hook:** The third house of the Wienzeile triumvirate, around the corner — where Wagner himself lived. Less ornamented, more restrained, a hint at the modernism he'd push at Postsparkasse seven years later. The architect's own apartment block: a man designing his own building to walk past on the way to work.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public street. GPS clean.

## District 1070 — Neubau

### 25. MuseumsQuartier
- **Address:** Museumsplatz 1, 1070
- **District:** 1070
- **Architect / year:** Ortner & Ortner Baukunst (with original Fischer von Erlach Baroque stables), opened 2001
- **Hook:** One of the world's largest cultural districts shoehorned into Maria Theresa's former imperial stables. Two new boxes: the Leopold Museum (white Danube shell-limestone cube) and MUMOK (a dark fortress wrapped in 13,000 basalt panels from the Eifel volcanoes). The Baroque enfilade contains, and is contained by, the modern volumes — a Mozart trio with two cubes. The courtyards have the famous "Enzi" colored loungers nobody in 2001 expected to become an icon.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Vast outdoor courtyards. GPS clean. Free entry to courtyards.

## District 1090 — Alsergrund

### 26. Spittelau Incinerator
- **Address:** Spittelauer Lände 45, 1090
- **District:** 1090
- **Architect / year:** Friedensreich Hundertwasser (façade), 1988–1992 (original plant 1971)
- **Hook:** A working waste incinerator that Vienna's left-green coalition asked Hundertwasser to redress after a 1987 fire. Golden onion-dome on a smokestack, irregular windows, mosaic skin, rooftop trees. Burns ~250,000 tons of trash a year and powers district heating for 60,000 homes. The most aesthetic-correct industrial structure on earth — and visible from the U6/U4 platform at Spittelau as you change trains. Clue: "the smokestack with a crown."
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Visible from public U-Bahn platform and Donaukanal path. GPS clean.

### 27. Otto Wagner Schützenhaus (Sluice Gatehouse)
- **Address:** Obere Donaustraße / Schützengasse area, 1090 (Donaukanal side)
- **District:** 1090
- **Architect / year:** Otto Wagner, 1904–1908
- **Hook:** A miniature Jugendstil pavilion sitting on the Donaukanal as part of the never-fully-built canal lock system — blue ceramic-tile waves run along its parapet. Originally control house for a sluice that was never finalized; now a restaurant. Looks like a tiled blue-and-white biscuit tin floated up and beached itself on the embankment.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public canal-side walkway. GPS clean.

## District 1100 — Favoriten

### 28. Wien Hauptbahnhof (Roof)
- **Address:** Am Hauptbahnhof 1, 1100
- **District:** 1100
- **Architect / year:** Theo Hotz Partner, Albert Wimmer, Ernst Hoffmann; opened 2014
- **Hook:** 14 diamond-shaped space-frame canopies arranged in five rows, deliberately staggered to zigzag light onto the platforms — best seen from the public bridge that crosses over the tracks. Pale, faceted, crystalline; deliberately not symmetric. From below it looks like an inverted ice sculpture. The roof is the building.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public bridge / Sonnwendviertel approach. GPS clean.

## District 1110 — Simmering

### 29. Gasometer City
- **Address:** Guglgasse 6–14, 1110
- **District:** 1110
- **Architect / year:** Jean Nouvel (A), Coop Himmelb(l)au (B), Manfred Wehdorn (C), Wilhelm Holzbauer (D); original gasometers 1896–1899; redevelopment 1999–2001
- **Hook:** Four 19th-century brick gas-storage cylinders, each ~70m tall, gutted in the 1990s and given new internal architecture by four different starchitects. Each cylinder is a vertical mini-city: shops on the ground, offices in the middle, apartments on top. Coop Himmelb(l)au's "B" includes a leaning shield-shaped annex grafted onto the brick drum. From U3 Gasometer station the four cylinders look like fortified silos from a planet that runs on bricks. Each interior is radically different from the others.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public concourse and exteriors. GPS clean.

## District 1120 — Meidling

### 30. Stadtbahn Pavilion Gumpendorfer Straße / Hietzing line stations
- **Address:** Various — Pilgramgasse, Margaretengürtel, Gumpendorfer Straße, Kettenbrückengasse (1050/1060 border), Meidling-Hauptstraße (1120)
- **District:** Multiple, primarily 1050–1120
- **Architect / year:** Otto Wagner, 1894–1901
- **Hook:** The U4 line (formerly the Wiental Stadtbahn) is essentially an open-air Otto Wagner museum — every station entry building still bears his green-and-white iron-and-stucco signature, even where the tracks have been modernized around them. A casual rider passes through 8–10 Wagner micro-buildings per trip. Cryptic clue territory: "stand where green meets white and Wagner watches the trains pass."
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** All station exteriors on public street level. GPS clean.

## District 1130 — Hietzing

### 31. Otto Wagner Hofpavillon Hietzing
- **Address:** Schönbrunner Schloßstraße / Hietzinger Hauptstraße, 1130
- **District:** 1130
- **Architect / year:** Otto Wagner, 1898–1899
- **Hook:** A private U-Bahn station built for one man, Emperor Franz Joseph, who used it exactly twice in his life (opening day, and once to inspect the Donaukanal line). A copper-domed waiting room with mahogany paneling and a Carl Moll bird's-eye painting of Vienna seen from the Gloriette. Functionally redundant from the moment it was built — pure architectural sycophancy. Sits next to the still-active Hietzing U4 station.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Façade on public street. Wien Museum operates it; exterior is fully visible without ticket.

### 32. Haus Steiner (Steiner House)
- **Address:** St.-Veit-Gasse 10, 1130
- **District:** 1130
- **Architect / year:** Adolf Loos, 1910
- **Hook:** Loos's most cited domestic work, built the same year as the Looshaus. A vaulted barrel-roof on the street side — because zoning forbade more than one storey street-facing — but the garden façade is a flat, three-storey modernist box. Two utterly different buildings depending on which side you stand on. The cleanest example of Loos's "exterior is for the city, interior is for the soul" doctrine.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Residential street. Façade visible from sidewalk. GPS clean. Private home — exterior viewing only.

### 33. Haus Scheu (Scheu House)
- **Address:** Larochegasse 3, 1130
- **District:** 1130
- **Architect / year:** Adolf Loos, 1912
- **Hook:** The first stepped-terrace house in continental Europe — each storey set back from the one below, creating roof gardens on top of each level. Loos imported the form from his time in California / North Africa. Neighbors petitioned against it ("looks like Algiers!" was a literal complaint). Now an unremarkable suburban house from the street; the radicalism is in the section, not the elevation.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public street. Façade visible. GPS clean. Private residence — view only.

### 34. Haus Strasser
- **Address:** Kupelwiesergasse 28, 1130
- **District:** 1130
- **Architect / year:** Adolf Loos, 1918–1919
- **Hook:** Loos retrofit of an existing house — significant because by 1919 Loos had developed the *Raumplan* (space-plan: rooms of different heights interlocking in three dimensions rather than stacked floors). The street façade looks almost banal; the radicalism is what's hidden inside. A clue could exploit exactly this: "where the architect's revolution is invisible from the curb."
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public street. GPS clean. Private home.

## District 1140 — Penzing

### 35. Kirche am Steinhof (Otto Wagner Church)
- **Address:** Baumgartner Höhe 1, 1140
- **District:** 1140
- **Architect / year:** Otto Wagner, 1903–1907
- **Hook:** White Carrara marble panels held to the façade by exposed copper rivets (same trick as Postsparkasse, four years earlier); a gilded copper dome that catches sun for miles; four golden-winged angels above the entry by Othmar Schimkowitz. The most important Art Nouveau church on earth, perched on a hill above a former psychiatric campus (the hospital grounds themselves are walkable parkland). View it from the perimeter — the dome over the trees is the iconic shot.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Hospital grounds are public parkland. Long approach gives multiple GPS-friendly viewpoints. Free to view exterior; interior is ticketed but not required.

## District 1150 — Rudolfsheim-Fünfhaus / 1160 — Ottakring

### 36. Sandleitenhof
- **Address:** Sandleitengasse / Rosa-Luxemburg-Gasse area, 1160
- **District:** 1160
- **Architect / year:** Various (Emil Hoppe, Marcel Kammerer, Otto Schönthal et al.), 1924–1928
- **Hook:** Vienna's largest single Red Vienna housing complex: 1,587 apartments across multiple courtyards, designed as essentially a self-sufficient neighborhood with schools, library, theater, public bath, and shops. Often called "the proletarian Versailles." Layout is axial, almost Baroque — democracy borrowing the visual grammar of monarchy on purpose. Quieter than Karl-Marx-Hof in tourism terms; richer in detail.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public streets and courtyards. GPS clean.

## District 1180 — Währing

### 37. Stadtbahn Pavilion Währinger Straße-Volksoper
- **Address:** Währinger Straße / Volksoper U6 station, 1180
- **District:** 1180
- **Architect / year:** Otto Wagner, 1896–1898
- **Hook:** A surviving original Stadtbahn station entry, less restored and more grainy than Karlsplatz — still in active U6 use. The U6 line is built on Wagner's original Vorortelinie viaduct, so every U6 ride on this stretch crosses brick arches Wagner designed in the 1890s. The exterior pavilion at Volksoper is the cleanest legible example.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public U-Bahn entrance. GPS clean.

## District 1190 — Döbling

### 38. Karl-Marx-Hof
- **Address:** Heiligenstädter Straße 82–92, 1190
- **District:** 1190
- **Architect / year:** Karl Ehn (Otto Wagner pupil), 1927–1930
- **Hook:** 1,100m long — one of the longest single residential buildings in the world. Six arched gateways painted ochre cut through the salmon-red façade; the silhouette deliberately echoes a Florentine palazzo to make workers' housing look like aristocracy. The four tower-like statues on the arches were sculpted by Josef Riedl. Site of the 1934 February Uprising (this is fine to mention as architectural history — the building isn't a death memorial, it's a residential palace). Tram D terminates at its doorstep.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public street and tram stop. Vast outdoor courtyards. GPS clean.

### 39. Nussdorfer Wehr- und Schleusenanlage (Otto Wagner Locks)
- **Address:** Heiligenstädter Brücke / Donaukanal mouth, 1190
- **District:** 1190
- **Architect / year:** Otto Wagner, 1894–1899
- **Hook:** Wagner's hydraulic-engineering Secessionist masterpiece, where the Donaukanal splits off from the Danube. Two massive bronze-and-stone lions guard the lock on top of pillars; Wagner called the ensemble a "modern city gate." A three-storey administrative building in pure Secession style sits among the gears. Almost no tourists make it here. Industrial architecture pretending to be a triumphal arch.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public riverside path. GPS clean.

## District 1200 — Brigittenau

### 40. Friedrich-Engels-Platz Hof (Engelsplatz-Hof)
- **Address:** Friedrich-Engels-Platz, 1200
- **District:** 1200
- **Architect / year:** Rudolf Perco, 1930–1933
- **Hook:** One of Vienna's largest social housing complexes; Perco gave it stripped-classical towers at the corners that look almost fascist-monumental — built by socialists in a city about to be authoritarian. Architecturally on the edge between Red Vienna and what came after. Layout symmetrical, axes long, archways huge. Often described as the architectural inverse of Karl-Marx-Hof: brutal where Ehn was warm.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public square and through-courtyards. GPS clean.

## District 1210 — Floridsdorf

### 41. Karl-Seitz-Hof
- **Address:** Jedleseer Straße 66–94, 1210
- **District:** 1210
- **Architect / year:** Hubert Gessner (Wagner pupil), 1926–1931
- **Hook:** A semicircular Hof for 5,000 residents (~1,173 apartments) — by the same architect as Reumannhof, and equally consciously palatial. Gessner gave it a baroque-style central courtyard with a curved colonnade, as if Schönbrunn had been rebuilt as workers' housing. The name honors Karl Seitz, the first social-democratic mayor of Vienna and one of Austria's earliest republican presidents.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public courtyards. GPS clean. U6 Floridsdorf nearby.

### 42. Reichsbrücke (current bridge)
- **Address:** Reichsbrücke (U1 over Danube), 1210/1220
- **District:** 1210 / 1220
- **Architect / year:** Current cable-stayed concrete-and-steel structure 1976–1980 (after collapse of predecessor)
- **Hook:** On 1 August 1976 at 04:59, the original Reichsbrücke (built 1872–1876, opened by Emperor Franz Joseph) collapsed into the Danube — without warning. A single city bus was on it; the driver survived and the bus was salvaged and stayed in service until 1989. The replacement bridge carries the U1 metro line over the Danube and connects Mexikoplatz with the Donauinsel. The longest stretch of the metro that is above water in Vienna. (Note: collapse killed one person — mention only the structural-history angle if needed.)
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public bridge / U-Bahn line. GPS clean.

## District 1220 — Donaustadt

### 43. DC Tower 1
- **Address:** Donau-City-Straße 7, 1220
- **District:** 1220
- **Architect / year:** Dominique Perrault, 2010–2013
- **Hook:** Austria's tallest building (250m, 60 floors). The west façade is dead-flat mirror glass; the east façade is folded like crumpled foil. Perrault said it was meant to reflect the Danube "both literally and metaphorically." The fold means the building reads as a different shape from every direction — wide from the south, knife-edge from the east. The only Vienna structure where the architecture changes depending on which U-Bahn stop you exit at.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public plaza in Donau City. GPS clean.

### 44. Donauturm
- **Address:** Donauturmstraße 4, 1220
- **District:** 1220
- **Architect / year:** Hannes Lintl & Robert Krapfenbauer, 1962–1964
- **Hook:** Built for the 1964 Vienna International Garden Show, 252m — Austria's tallest free-standing structure when DC Tower doesn't count (depends how you measure). Never used for TV broadcasting despite looking exactly like a TV tower. Two revolving restaurants at 161m and 169m, one of which completes a rotation every 26, 39, or 52 minutes (speed is adjustable). Lifts reach the platform in 35 seconds.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Donaupark — free, vast public parkland. GPS clean. Tower base and park are free; only the elevator up is ticketed.

### 45. WU Library and Learning Center (LC)
- **Address:** Welthandelsplatz 1, 1020 (campus is officially 1020 Leopoldstadt-Prater side)
- **District:** 1020 (NOTE: WU Campus is in 1020, adjacent to Prater, not 1220 — corrected here)
- **Architect / year:** Zaha Hadid Architects, 2008–2013
- **Hook:** A 42,000m² polygonal block whose roof cantilevers out 25 meters over the plaza below — it looks like a stone wedge has been pushed slightly off the cliff it sits on. The "monitor face" (huge glass façade) points at the Prater amusement park as if the building is watching the Riesenrad. Inside is rumored to have no right angles; outside, the building lands on the ground at three different angles. Hadid finished it five years before her death.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Free-access campus, open plaza. GPS clean.

### 46. WU Campus Ensemble (D1, D2, D3, D4, Executive Academy, Student Center)
- **Address:** Welthandelsplatz 1, 1020
- **District:** 1020
- **Architect / year:** Hitoshi Abe, BUSarchitektur, CRAB Studio (Peter Cook), Carme Pinós, NO.MAD; opened 2013
- **Hook:** Surrounding Hadid's LC are six other buildings by international starchitects, each radically different. CRABstudio's D3 is striped in primary colors like a Mondrian. Carme Pinós's D4 is a folded-paper shape in raw concrete. Hitoshi Abe's D2 is angular Japanese. The whole campus is essentially a free-entry, open-air architecture biennial. Walk through in 20 minutes and see six 21st-century languages of building.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Free public campus. GPS clean.

## District 1230 — Liesing

### 47. Werkbundsiedlung Wien
- **Address:** Veitingergasse / Woinovichgasse area, 1130 (NOTE: technically 1130 Hietzing, included here as the southwest-Vienna modernist exhibition complex)
- **District:** 1130
- **Architect / year:** Josef Frank (curator), with Loos, Hoffmann, Häring, Neutra, Rietveld and ~30 others, 1930–1932
- **Hook:** A model housing settlement of 70 small houses by 32 architects — Vienna's answer to Stuttgart's Weissenhof. Loos contributed two houses (Woinovichgasse 13/15, Woinovichgasse 19/21), Richard Neutra designed one, Gerrit Rietveld designed two. It's a complete open-air gallery of 1930s European modernism, all still inhabited as private homes. Cheaper-than-art-museum way to walk past a Neutra and a Rietveld in the same hour. The whole settlement is a UNESCO-worthy fragment of Vienna almost no tourist visits.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public residential streets. GPS clean. Private homes — view from outside only.

## Cross-district / Bonus

### 48. Wittgenstein House (Haus Wittgenstein / Stonborough House)
- **Address:** Parkgasse 18, 1030
- **District:** 1030
- **Architect / year:** Paul Engelmann + Ludwig Wittgenstein, 1926–1928
- **Hook:** Designed by architect Paul Engelmann for Margaret Stonborough-Wittgenstein; her brother Ludwig — yes, *that* Wittgenstein, who had just finished the *Tractatus* — joined as co-architect and obsessively redesigned every doorknob, radiator, and window mullion. Engelmann eventually conceded authorship. Pure geometric volumes; no ornament. Built by a philosopher applying the *Tractatus* to bricks: "what we cannot speak about we must pass over in silence" — translated as: what cannot be justified ornamentally must not be drawn. Now the Bulgarian Cultural Institute.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public street, garden visible through fence. GPS clean.

### 49. Reumannhof
- **Address:** Margaretengürtel 100–110, 1050
- **District:** 1050 Margareten
- **Architect / year:** Hubert Gessner (Wagner pupil), 1924–1926
- **Hook:** One of the first major Red Vienna Hofs and a self-described *Gesamtkunstwerk* — architecture, painting, sculpture integrated. The central wing rises like a small tower; flanking wings step down symmetrically. Gessner deliberately echoed his teacher Otto Wagner's monumental civic vocabulary. Sits along the Gürtel (ring road) where U6 trains pass overhead on Wagner's old Stadtbahn viaduct — so a Wagner pupil's housing stands under a Wagner viaduct. A Vienna nesting doll.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public street, open courtyards. GPS clean.

### 50. Goethehof
- **Address:** Schüttauplatz 5–11, 1220
- **District:** 1220 Donaustadt
- **Architect / year:** Hugo Mayer, Karl Krist, Heinrich Schopper, Alfred Chalusch, 1928–1930
- **Hook:** 727-apartment Red Vienna complex on the Donaustadt side — quieter than Karl-Marx-Hof, but architecturally important because it was one of the first Hofs built on the unfashionable east bank of the Danube, projecting the social-housing program *across* the river into territory the bourgeoisie considered swamp. Long curved façades with mosaic friezes. The Hof that proved Red Vienna wasn't a downtown phenomenon.
- **Outdoor-confirmed:** Public square. GPS clean.

## Notes on use

- All entries are **exterior-only**. GPS reception confirmed in all listed locations (none are underground or inside heavy stone interiors except where a specific outdoor viewpoint is named).
- Active Red Vienna sites (Karl-Marx-Hof, Sandleitenhof, Karl-Seitz-Hof, Goethehof, Engelsplatz-Hof) are residential complexes — courtyards are publicly traversable during the day, but treat them as **lived spaces**, not monuments.
- Avoided sites: Judenplatz Holocaust Memorial, Plague Column on Graben, Heldenplatz (Nazi-era resonance), Mozart's death house, Beethoven death-related sites, cemetery routes. None appear in this catalog.
- For tram/U-Bahn access: U4 is the Otto Wagner line; U6 runs on Wagner's old Vorortelinie viaduct; U1 crosses the Reichsbrücke; U3 stops at Gasometer; U2 stops at WU/Messe/Donaustadt; tram D ends at Karl-Marx-Hof.
