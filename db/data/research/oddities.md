# Vienna Oddities, Curiosities & Urban Legends

Research catalog for the RubyConf Austria 2026 treasure hunt (May 29–31, Vienna).
All entries are outdoor, free to physically reach, GPS-friendly, U-Bahn / tram accessible.
Curated to skip Holocaust / Nazi / Jewish-trauma sites, plague-corpse and execution memorials, composer deathbed sites, and morbid burial spots. Bias is toward the weird, the surprising, the layered, and the "wait, what is that?" detail almost no one notices.

District codes follow Vienna's 1010–1230 convention. Address resolves to the visible exterior — entry is never required.

---

## 1010 — Innere Stadt (Old Town)

### 1. Stock im Eisen (The Nail Tree)
- **Address:** Stock-im-Eisen-Platz, corner of Graben & Kärntner Straße (Palais Equitable)
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A 1440-felled spruce trunk wrapped in five iron bands and bristling with hundreds of forged nails — every wandering locksmith and journeyman passing through Vienna pounded one in for luck. Legend says the Devil himself put the tree in irons, or that an apprentice sold his soul for an unbreakable lock. Tree-ring science confirms the wood is medieval. It now stands behind glass at a building corner that millions walk past without ever looking up.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Visible from the street through a glass case at the building corner. GPS open.

### 2. Türkenkugel (Stephansdom Turkish Cannonball)
- **Address:** Stephansplatz, embedded in a south-side buttress of Stephansdom
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A 1683 Ottoman cannonball lodged in the cathedral wall and never removed — they just dated it. One of perhaps a thousand fired at the spire during the Second Turkish Siege. The clue isn't the cathedral; it's the small dark sphere most pilgrims walk past without noticing.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Buttress visible from the square. No entry required.

### 3. Riesentor & the Mammoth Bone Story
- **Address:** Stephansplatz, west front of Stephansdom (Giant's Gate)
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** In 1443, workers digging Stephansdom's foundation hit a colossal thighbone. They hung it above the door, certain it belonged to one of the giants who built the world. It was a mammoth. The bone now lives at the University's Geological Institute — but the gate kept the name "Riesentor" (Giant's Gate). Inscribed on it: 1443, and Frederick III's never-quite-decoded motto **AEIOU**.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. The portal is street-side; no ticket required.

### 4. The Heidentürme (Pagan Towers)
- **Address:** West façade of Stephansdom, flanking the Riesentor
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Two stubby Romanesque towers that don't match the soaring Gothic spire. The name "Heathen Towers" comes either from being built of Roman rubble, or because the silhouette resembled minarets — or because they actually sit on a pagan temple site. The oldest part of the cathedral, hidden in plain sight beneath the more famous south tower's shadow.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Visible from Stephansplatz.

### 5. The "05" Carving on Stephansdom
- **Address:** Stephansplatz, right of the Riesentor (Giant's Gate), under glass
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A small cipher — O5 — scratched into the cathedral wall. The "O" plus "5" = Ö+E = "Österreich." A wartime resistance code, cut by Lt-Col Mühlfeit during the 1945 battle for Vienna and re-inscribed permanently in 1965. The hook is the cryptography itself: a name written by counting letters. Decide whether the wartime undertone is acceptable; the layered code-puzzle angle is the strongest draw.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Behind a small glass plate on the exterior wall.

### 6. Schönlaterngasse (Street of the Beautiful Lantern)
- **Address:** Schönlaterngasse, between Sonnenfelsgasse and Postgasse
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A curved Baroque alley named in 1780 after a single ornate iron lantern at No. 6 (the original is in the Wien Museum; a 1971 replica hangs in its place). The street bends because it traces a medieval property line, and the whole alley feels like it belongs in a different city.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Open public alley.

### 7. Basiliskenhaus (Basilisk House)
- **Address:** Schönlaterngasse 7
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** On 26 June 1212, a baker's apprentice in this courtyard found a basilisk — rooster-headed serpent with a fatal stare — at the bottom of the well. He killed it by holding up a mirror. The façade carries a stone basilisk and a 1577 inscription re-cut in 1932. Geologist Eduard Suess later proposed the real cause was toxic gas in the well, which is somehow even better. The plaque is at street level; you can stand and read it.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Façade plaque and carving visible from the alley.

### 8. Heiligenkreuzerhof
- **Address:** Entrances at Schönlaterngasse 5 and Grashofgasse 3
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A silent Baroque courtyard owned since the 12th century by the Cistercian Abbey of Heiligenkreuz — an outpost of a forest monastery sitting in the middle of Vienna's inner city. Walk in through one alley, walk out a different one. The Bernardikapelle (1679) sits in the corner. Almost no tourists know it exists.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Open Durchhaus (pass-through courtyard).

### 9. Donnerbrunnen (Providence Fountain)
- **Address:** Neuer Markt
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Georg Raphael Donner's 1739 fountain has four allegorical rivers of the Habsburg lands sitting around it — naked. Empress Maria Theresa was so scandalized in 1770 she ordered them removed and melted down. They survived (now in the Wien Museum). The bronze copies on the square today look serene; few visitors know they once nearly went into the foundry.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Open square, GPS clean.

### 10. Pestsäule (Plague Column / Trinity Column)
- **Address:** Graben
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A 1694 Baroque pile of cherubs, clouds and gilded sunbursts on Vienna's main pedestrian street. The hook isn't the plague — it's the iconography: a kneeling Habsburg with the family's famous jutting jaw, and "Faith" defeating an upside-down hag who represents pestilence. The whole column is essentially a politicized PR monument disguised as religious art. (If plague theme is too close to the avoid-list, skip — but the visual is more triumphal than morbid.)
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Centre of Graben.

### 11. Naglergasse — the Roman Wall Curve
- **Address:** Naglergasse, off Graben toward Freyung
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A short street that bends sharply for no obvious reason. The bend is the south-west corner of a Roman military camp (Vindobona) that vanished 1,800 years ago. The trench in front of the wall is now a street called Graben ("ditch"). Walking Naglergasse is walking along a Roman fortification you cannot see.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Public street.

### 12. Heidenschuß
- **Address:** Heidenschuß, off Freyung
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** "Heathen Shot" — named for an old house sign showing a Saracen on horseback firing arrows. Origin story: during the 1529 Ottoman siege, a baker's apprentice supposedly heard Turks tunneling beneath his cellar and raised the alarm. Probably folklore, but the carved Turk on horseback was the city's reminder for 300 years. The current figure replaces the original 1851 version.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. House-sign visible at street level.

### 13. Ankeruhr (Anchor Clock)
- **Address:** Hoher Markt 10–11 (spanning Bauernmarkt)
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A 1914 Jugendstil clock-bridge by Franz von Matsch (a close associate of Klimt's). Twelve historical Viennese figures rotate past, one per hour, beginning with Marcus Aurelius. At noon, all twelve parade with music. It's basically a Habsburg cuckoo clock the size of a building, hidden in an arch.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Visible from the square below.

### 14. Roman Vindobona at Hoher Markt
- **Address:** Hoher Markt (around the square)
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Below this square sit the foundations of a Roman officer's house (uncovered during 1948–49 sewer work). The street layout itself encodes the camp: Graben, Naglergasse, Tiefer Graben, Salzgries, Rabensteig, Rotenturmstraße all trace its outer wall. Above ground there are several brass plaques and outlines marking where the legion stood. The legion that camped here was the X Gemina.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Markers visible at street level on the square.

### 15. Michaelerplatz Roman Excavations
- **Address:** Centre of Michaelerplatz
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A sunken pit in the middle of one of Vienna's grandest squares, exposing Roman roads and medieval wall foundations 2,000 years deep. Discovered during 1990–91 renovations and left open. You walk around the rim and look down through layered centuries.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Open-air archaeological exposure, ringed by railings.

### 16. Looshaus ("the House Without Eyebrows")
- **Address:** Michaelerplatz 3
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** In 1909, Adolf Loos built the most controversial façade in Vienna directly opposite the Hofburg: no ornamentation, no window crowns, smooth as a slab. Emperor Franz Joseph allegedly called it "the house without eyebrows" and refused to use the matching palace gate ever again. Loos eventually appeased the city by adding flower boxes. It's now considered the birth-cry of architectural modernism.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Façade visible directly from Michaelerplatz.

### 17. Schweizertor (Swiss Gate)
- **Address:** Hofburg, in der Burg → Schweizerhof
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A blood-red and ink-black Renaissance gate from 1552, the oldest portal of the Hofburg. The inscription lists every title Ferdinand I claimed in the year of the gate (Roman numerals MDLII). Despite the name, no Swiss soldier saw it until 200 years later under Maria Theresa. It looks more like Florence than Vienna.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Walk through it freely.

### 18. The Stallburg
- **Address:** Reitschulgasse / Habsburgergasse (between Michaelerplatz and Josefsplatz)
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A 1560s Renaissance palazzo wedged inside an otherwise Baroque/Rococo city — and 72 Lipizzaner stallions still live on its ground floor. From the walkway you can sometimes see the horses' heads. The most expensively-housed mammals in Vienna live in a 460-year-old building.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Exterior and walkway are public.

### 19. The Spanish Riding School façade (from Josefsplatz)
- **Address:** Josefsplatz (Hofburg)
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Fischer von Erlach's white riding hall (1729) — Europe's only place where classical dressage has been performed continuously since the 16th century. From Josefsplatz, you're standing in the spot where the school's predecessor (a wooden arena) used to be.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. The square is fully accessible.

### 20. Kapuzinergruft entrance door
- **Address:** Tegetthoffstraße 2 (side door of Capuchin Church)
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** No going inside. The hook is the door itself and a Habsburg burial ritual: when a Habsburg's coffin arrives, the master of ceremonies knocks three times. The Capuchin asks: *"Wer begehrt Einlass?"* ("Who requests entry?"). The titles are recited; the monk refuses. The titles are stripped; refused again. Only on the third try, when the candidate is named as "a poor sinner," is the door opened. The church facade is austere by Capuchin vow — deliberately humble next door to imperial Vienna.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Door and façade visible from the street. (Carol Reed shot a scene of *The Third Man* here.)

### 21. Augustinerkirche & the Herzgruft hint
- **Address:** Augustinerstraße 3, Josefsplatz
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Stand outside this church and you're standing above 54 silver urns containing Habsburg hearts. Body to the Capuchins, heart to the Augustinians, intestines to Stephansdom's catacombs — the Habsburgs literally split themselves across three Vienna buildings. The Herzgruft (1654–1878) is closed except on All Souls' Day. The church looks plain on Josefsplatz; the secret is what lies behind one wall.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Façade-only is fine.

### 22. Loos American Bar (exterior)
- **Address:** Kärntner Durchgang 10
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Tucked into a shopping passage off Kärntner Straße is a 27-square-metre bar Adolf Loos built in 1908. The room is so small a mirror trick makes it feel infinite. From outside it's almost a slot in the wall — the easy-to-miss American-flag-coloured panel above the door. Vienna's smallest world-famous building.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Passage and facade are public.

### 23. Mozarthaus / Figarohaus (exterior)
- **Address:** Domgasse 5
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Mozart's only surviving Vienna apartment. He wrote *The Marriage of Figaro* here (1784–87). It was the biggest, most expensive flat he ever lived in. The exterior plaque is unassuming; the alley still looks 18th-century. Domgasse runs behind Stephansdom in a hush.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Façade and plaque only.

### 24. Schreyvogelgasse 8 — the Third Man doorway
- **Address:** Schreyvogelgasse 8, actually on Mölker Steig
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** The doorway where Orson Welles steps out of shadow, a cat at his shoelaces (pilchard oil bait), in arguably cinema's most famous reveal (*The Third Man*, 1949). The doorway is unchanged, repainted brighter. Linklater later used it in *Before Sunrise*. A film-history Easter egg hiding on a tiny side street.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Public alley.

### 25. Pasqualatihaus & the Mölker Bastei
- **Address:** Mölker Bastei 8
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A block of houses perched on a surviving fragment of Vienna's 16th-century city wall. The bastion is still there under your feet. Beethoven lived on the 4th floor here on and off between 1804 and 1814, composing his 4th, 5th, and 7th Symphonies and *Fidelio*. The walls are literal fortification stone.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Exterior and ramp are public.

### 26. Maria am Gestade ("Mary on the Bank")
- **Address:** Salvatorgasse 12
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A delicate Gothic church on a hill that used to be a Danube riverbank — until the river was diverted and the bank became dry land. The nave is built skewed because the ground was too tight. The 56-metre openwork tower (1419–1428) is unique in Vienna. Napoleon's army turned it into a horse stable in 1809. From outside, it looks like Gothic lace.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Façade and tower view from Salvatorgasse.

### 27. Ruprechtskirche — Vienna's oldest church
- **Address:** Ruprechtsplatz, near Schwedenplatz
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A small Romanesque church traditionally dated to ~800 AD, patron saint of the Vienna salt merchants. The bells (c. 1280) are the oldest in the city. The whole building looks too small and too old to belong in the modern city; ivy creeps up the wall. It sits on the edge of the Bermuda Triangle nightlife zone, so the contrast at dusk is wild.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Façade and surrounding square.

### 28. The Bermuda Triangle (Bermudadreieck)
- **Address:** Triangle bounded by Seitenstettengasse, Rabensteig and Sterngasse, near Schwedenplatz
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A medieval street tangle so disorientating that locals coined the nickname after the Atlantic Triangle: come in, get lost, vanish. The 1980s reinvented the area as Vienna's bar-crawl maze. By day, it's silent old town; by night, it eats itself. Streets look almost identical.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Open streets, GPS works (when you have a signal — that's literally the joke).

### 29. Schemerl Bridge / Otto Wagner Donaukanal Sluice
- **Address:** Nussdorf, where the Donaukanal splits from the Danube (Brigittenauer Brücke area; tram D end)
- **District:** 1010 / 1190 border
- **Hook:** A 1898 Otto Wagner weir + bridge complex that controls where the Donaukanal branches off the main river. Two bronze lions guard the gates. Hardly anyone visits, but it's the most monumental piece of waterworks infrastructure in the city — civic engineering treated as imperial sculpture.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Bridge and overlook are public.

### 30. Globe Museum & Esperanto Museum (Palais Mollard exterior)
- **Address:** Herrengasse 9
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** One late-17th-century palace, two of the world's only specialised museums: 240 antique terrestrial and celestial globes (including Moon and Mars), and a museum dedicated to artificial languages (Esperanto, Volapük, Klingon). The façade gives no clue what's inside. The address is on Vienna's haughtiest old street.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Façade plaque references the collections.

### 31. Secession Building & the "Golden Cabbage"
- **Address:** Friedrichstraße 12
- **District:** 1010 (border 1040)
- **Hook:** Olbrich's 1898 art temple, instantly nicknamed "the golden cabbage" (Krauthappel) because the dome of 2,500 gilded laurel leaves resembled a vegetable. Above the door: *"Der Zeit ihre Kunst. Der Kunst ihre Freiheit"* ("To every age its art. To art, its freedom.") Klimt's Beethoven Frieze is inside, but the exterior alone is a Jugendstil manifesto.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Dome and inscription visible from the street.

### 32. Goethe & Schiller — the staring contest
- **Address:** Goethe statue at Opernring; Schiller statue across Burgring in Schillerpark
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Two German literary giants, lifelong friends, sit in stone about 180 metres apart on opposite sides of the Ringstraße — and they're staring directly at each other. Goethe languid and seated (unveiled 1900). Schiller upright on a column (1876). Walk Schiller's line of sight and you find Goethe. Vienna's longest unblinking literary eye-contact.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Both monuments are open Ring traffic islands.

### 33. Mozart Statue + Treble Clef Flowerbed
- **Address:** Burggarten, near the Goethe statue
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A 7.5-metre Mozart in the Burggarten — but the real curiosity is at his feet: a flowerbed of red blossoms shaped into a giant treble clef. In bloom (May, June) it's almost ridiculous in how earnest it is. Sculpted 1896 by Tilgner; bombed in 1945; moved here in 1953.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Open park.

### 34. Karl Lueger Monument — the 3.5-degree tilt
- **Address:** Dr.-Karl-Lueger-Platz (near Stubentor)
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Vienna's most contested statue. Lueger (mayor 1897–1910) was an antisemite admired by Hitler. Rather than remove him, the city ran a competition and selected artist Klemens Wihlidal's intervention: tilt the entire statue and pedestal 3.5° to the right. Construction began January 2026; completion summer 2026 — your hunt will see it actively tilted or just-finished. A monument literally off-balance.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Public square.

### 35. Donauweibchen Brunnen (Danube Mermaid Fountain)
- **Address:** Stadtpark, near the Wienflussportal
- **District:** 1010 (border 1030)
- **Hook:** Vienna has a mermaid. Hanns Gasser's 1862 figure is the city's Donauweibchen — a friendly river-woman from Viennese folklore who'd warn fishermen of floods and bring treasure from the riverbed. The original marble was bombed in WWII; this is a 1948 stone copy. Find her tucked among bushes near where the Wien river emerges from underground.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Park.

### 36. Wienflusseinwölbung / Wienflussportal (the buried Wien river emerging)
- **Address:** Stadtpark, by the Wienfluss promenade
- **District:** 1010 / 1030
- **Hook:** The Wien river runs underground through much of the inner city; in Stadtpark it surfaces, framed by Friedrich Ohmann's 1906 Jugendstil archway with cobalt-blue ceramic vases. You can stand directly above where a river vanishes and reappears. Below the arch, the U4 thunders past on Otto Wagner's Stadtbahn line.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Park promenade.

### 37. Otto Wagner Stadtbahn Pavilions (Karlsplatz)
- **Address:** Karlsplatz (both sides)
- **District:** 1010 / 1040
- **Hook:** Two 1898 Art-Nouveau metro stations — steel frames clad in marble slabs, sunflower motifs, apple-green and white. Built when Vienna decided even subway entrances should be art. One is a café; one is an exhibition space. They were dismantled and lifted 1.5 m above their original ground level in 1977.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Open plaza.

### 38. Tegetthoff Column — the landlocked admiral
- **Address:** Praterstern
- **District:** 1020
- **Hook:** A monumental column topped by a bronze admiral — for a country with no coastline today. Wilhelm von Tegetthoff smashed the Italian navy at the Battle of Lissa (1866) when Austria-Hungary still had a fleet. The column is wrapped in six bronze ramming-prows from captured ships, a Roman *columna rostrata* style. Vienna's landlocked naval monument: a 600-km inland tribute to a sea victory.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Centre of the Praterstern roundabout.

## 1020 — Leopoldstadt

### 39. Republic of Kugelmugel
- **Address:** Antifaschismusplatz 2, Prater
- **District:** 1020
- **Hook:** A spherical wooden house built by Edwin Lipburger in 1971 — declared its own micronation when Austrian authorities ruled spherical buildings were illegal. Lipburger issued passports, was jailed for fake stamps, was pardoned, was moved to Vienna's Prater in 1982, and surrounded with barbed wire. Reopened 2024 after restoration. Address: "2nd District, Anti-Fascism Square." A round, sovereign nation behind a fence in an amusement park.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Visible from the Hauptallee.

### 40. Wiener Riesenrad (Ferris Wheel from outside)
- **Address:** Riesenradplatz, Prater
- **District:** 1020
- **Hook:** Built 1897 by a British Royal Navy engineer (Walter Bassett). Survived a 1944 fire that destroyed most cars; only every second gondola was rebuilt — hence the strangely large gaps between cars today. Site of cinema's most famous monologue (Welles, *The Third Man*, 1949: "In Italy for 30 years under the Borgias they had warfare, terror, murder, bloodshed… they produced Michelangelo. In Switzerland they had brotherly love, 500 years of democracy and peace, and what did they produce? The cuckoo clock.").
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Plaza is free; no ticket needed for the view.

### 41. Augarten Porcelain Palace + Park Walls
- **Address:** Obere Augartenstraße 1
- **District:** 1020
- **Hook:** A 1705 Baroque pavilion in the middle of a French-formal park, still home to Europe's second-oldest porcelain manufactory (founded 1718). The original 18th-century park wall still encircles it. You can walk all the way around the perimeter and watch the wall — early 1700s, perfectly preserved — running for almost a kilometre.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Park is public; palace exterior fully visible.

## 1030 — Landstraße

### 42. Hundertwasserhaus
- **Address:** Kegelgasse 36–38 / Löwengasse
- **District:** 1030
- **Hook:** Friedensreich Hundertwasser believed straight lines were "godless." So in 1985 he built a public housing block with no right angles, undulating floors, mismatched windows, 200 trees growing out of the balconies, and an onion dome. It's an actual apartment building (people live there) — you can only view the exterior, which is the point. Look for the "tree tenants" sticking out of the façade.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Street view is the only view.

### 43. KunstHaus Wien (Hundertwasser Museum exterior)
- **Address:** Untere Weißgerberstraße 13
- **District:** 1030
- **Hook:** A former Thonet furniture factory (the bentwood chair people) that Hundertwasser rebuilt in 1989–91. Wobbly tile mosaics, a chessboard façade that refuses to be a chessboard, trees on the roof. Right around the corner from his housing block. Two Hundertwasser sites in one walk.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Façade is street-level.

## 1040 — Wieden

### 44. Karlskirche — Trajan-on-a-plague-vow
- **Address:** Karlsplatz
- **District:** 1040
- **Hook:** Fischer von Erlach's 1716 plague-vow church is architecturally insane in the best way: a Greek temple portico, a Byzantine dome, *and* two Roman triumphal columns flanking the entrance — explicit copies of Trajan's Column in Rome, except spiralled with the life of plague-saint Charles Borromeo instead of military victories. Plus two flanking pavilions that look Islamic. The Habsburgs literally collaged the world's empires into one façade. The reflecting pool with the Henry Moore sculpture is a 1978 addition.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Whole square is the view.

### 45. Otto Wagner Majolikahaus
- **Address:** Linke Wienzeile 40
- **District:** 1060
- **Hook:** A six-storey apartment block whose entire façade is glazed ceramic tiles, painted with floral vines that climb upward. Tiles, not paint — washable architecture (Wagner pointed out the practical advantage). Next door: Linke Wienzeile 38, the "Medallion House," with gilded stucco medallions and Schimkowitz's "Crying Women" sculptures on the roofline. Two-for-one Jugendstil hidden on a busy commercial street facing the Naschmarkt.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Sidewalk view.

## 1060 — Mariahilf

### 46. Esterházypark Flak Tower
- **Address:** Esterházypark / Fritz-Grünbaum-Platz
- **District:** 1060
- **Hook:** A 47-metre concrete cube of 3.5-metre-thick reinforced walls — too dense to demolish, so the city built an aquarium inside it (Haus des Meeres). On the outside, climbing routes go up the concrete and a glass-walled restaurant clings to the roof. A WWII anti-aircraft tower turned vertical zoo. The most brutally unkillable building in Vienna, surrounded by a park. (Note: WWII origin — but the "what to do with an indestructible bunker" angle is what makes it odd, not the war.)
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Park around the base.

## 1070 — Neubau

### 47. Spittelberg — the 18th-century pocket village
- **Address:** Spittelberggasse area, behind MuseumsQuartier
- **District:** 1070
- **Hook:** A cluster of preserved Biedermeier and 18th-century houses — narrow, cobbled, almost rural. Joseph II (Maria Theresa's son) reportedly visited the area incognito; one façade still bears (allegedly) a musket-ball mark from a Spittelberg brawl. The whole quarter survived two waves of city-clearing demolitions almost by accident.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Open streets.

## 1090 — Alsergrund

### 48. Strudlhofstiege
- **Address:** Between Liechtensteinstraße and Strudlhofgasse / Pasteurgasse
- **District:** 1090
- **Hook:** A 1910 Jugendstil outdoor staircase in luminescent Mannersdorf limestone, with terraces, fountains and lamps. Designed by Theodor Jaeger, completed in the same year Otto Wagner was building Stadtbahn stations. Heimito von Doderer made it the centrepiece of his 900-page 1951 novel *Die Strudlhofstiege*. A plaque with his opening poem is mounted at the foot of the stairs. A staircase that became literature.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Public stairway.

### 49. Berggasse 19 — Freud's address
- **Address:** Berggasse 19
- **District:** 1090
- **Hook:** Sigmund Freud lived and practised here 1891–1938. The couch left with him for London in 1938 (it's in the Freud Museum there). The Berggasse plaque is at street level. The hook for a riddle: this is where the modern idea of the unconscious mind was largely thought up — in an apartment that looks like every other Vienna apartment.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Façade and plaque.

## 1140 — Penzing

### 50. Kirche am Steinhof
- **Address:** Baumgartner Höhe 1
- **District:** 1140
- **Hook:** Otto Wagner's 1907 golden-domed Jugendstil church, perched on a hill in the middle of what was Vienna's psychiatric hospital complex. Wagner designed it specifically for mentally-ill patients: rounded corners (no head injuries on sharp stones), flowing-water fonts (no shared holy-water germs), separate doors for men and women, emergency exits behind the altar, almost no crucifixes. The dome is gilded with 2 kg of 0.01-mm-thin gold leaf. Architecture as therapy, hidden in a hospital wood.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Visible from outside the hospital grounds; gardens around it.

## 1190 — Döbling

### 51. Karl-Marx-Hof
- **Address:** Heiligenstädter Straße 82–92 (entrance arches along)
- **District:** 1190
- **Hook:** 1,100 metres long. The single longest residential building in the world. Built 1927–30 by Karl Ehn (an Otto Wagner pupil) under socialist "Red Vienna" — 1,382 apartments, kindergartens, libraries, a pharmacy, laundries, all financed by a luxury tax on the rich. The fortress-like archways were stormed by federal troops during the 1934 Austrian Civil War; bullet damage still visible in places. From the U4 you walk past four tram stops' worth of one building.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Entire exterior is public street.

### 52. Mayer am Pfarrplatz — Beethoven's Heiligenstadt house
- **Address:** Pfarrplatz 2
- **District:** 1190
- **Hook:** A wine tavern with a courtyard, blue Saint Florian statue at the corner, vines climbing the staircase. Beethoven lived here in 1817 trying to cure his deafness at the nearby spa. He failed. From the same Heiligenstadt summers came the *Heiligenstadt Testament* (1802) and ultimately the *Eroica*. The Pfarrplatz is tiny — three buildings, a church, a fountain. It feels like a village.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Square and façade.

## 1220 — Donaustadt

### 53. Donauturm (Danube Tower)
- **Address:** Donauturmstraße 4, Donaupark
- **District:** 1220
- **Hook:** 252 metres, Austria's tallest structure, opened 1964. The hook: it has two revolving restaurants stacked on top of each other (161 m and 169 m). Each does a full rotation in 24 minutes — about one cappuccino. From the top on a clear day you see the Hungarian border. Visible as a single needle from almost anywhere east of the Danube.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Visible from the surrounding park without paying.

## Bonus / Edge Cases

### 54. The Cabbage / "Grasshopper" sundial of Stephansdom (south-tower base)
- **Address:** Stephansplatz, south side of Stephansdom
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** Carved into the south wall near the tower base is a medieval unit-of-measure stone — the "Wiener Elle" (Vienna ell) — once used by traders to verify cloth lengths in public. Two iron bars sticking out, one for the linen ell, one for the woolen. The original municipal "you measured wrong" enforcement bureau, embedded in a cathedral wall.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Exterior wall at eye level.

### 55. Heidenturm Sundial on Stephansdom
- **Address:** South wall of Stephansdom
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A faded medieval sundial still painted on the cathedral wall. Most people look up at the spire; the sundial is at human height and almost invisible. Even when working, it was less accurate than the Ankeruhr a few streets away. A 600-year-old broken clock on the most famous Vienna building.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Exterior wall.

### 56. Drei-Mäderl-Haus (Three Maidens House)
- **Address:** Schreyvogelgasse 10
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A Biedermeier house immortalised in a 1916 operetta about Schubert and three sisters (totally fictional but enormously popular). The plaque on the wall is sentimental fluff — but the legend stuck so hard it shaped how Vienna remembered Schubert. Pure pop-culture-mythology applied to a quiet street. Right next door to the *Third Man* doorway (#24).
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Façade and plaque.

### 57. Heidenschuss horseman house-sign
- **Address:** Heidenschuß 3 corner
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** A wrought-iron Ottoman warrior on horseback, sabre raised, mounted on a corner wall. The current figure replaced a Saracen-archer from 1851. House-signs predate street numbers in Vienna; this is one of the few survivors. The whole tiny lane is named after this single carved figure.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Wall corner at street level.

### 58. Café Hawelka façade (Dorotheergasse 6)
- **Address:** Dorotheergasse 6
- **District:** 1010
- **Hook:** From outside, an unremarkable narrow shopfront. Inside (which you skip), the room was decorated 1939 by Rudolf Schindler — a former Otto Wagner and Adolf Loos student who later worked in Frank Lloyd Wright's Taliesin studio. The building was Vienna's first American Bar (1913, the "Chatham Bar"), pre-dating Loos's by a year. The exterior is a small portal in a back street that hides architectural pedigree.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Façade plaque.

### 59. Café Sperl façade (Gumpendorfer Straße 11)
- **Address:** Gumpendorfer Straße 11
- **District:** 1060
- **Hook:** A protected historic café from 1880 — but the hook is the exterior. The sign and the window frames are mostly original. The Sperl was the unofficial HQ of the *Hagenbund* artists' group around 1900; its billiard tables are still in the same room. From the street, time-capsule lettering.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. Façade and signage.

### 60. Schönbrunn Gloriette (long-range view)
- **Address:** Visible from the courtyard/garden entrance, Schönbrunner Schloßstraße 47
- **District:** 1130
- **Hook:** A purely-decorative triumphal arch built in 1775 by Maria Theresa to honor a Habsburg victory — that almost no one outside Austria can name (the Battle of Kolín, which they actually *lost* part of). A 30-metre arcade with no roof, purely there to be looked at from across the gardens. Possibly the most Habsburg object in Vienna: monumental, ornate, structurally useless.
- **Outdoor:** Yes. The grounds are free; the Gloriette is visible without entering the palace.
